How this helps your speaking

  1. Choose a discussion set below — each one has four broader questions linked to a Part 2-style topic, just like a real Part 3 discussion.
  2. Pick Practice Mode (tips visible, up to two minutes to answer) while you're learning the format, or Exam Mode (tips hidden, strict 45-second limit per answer) to test yourself properly.
  3. Tap the microphone to record your answer, then play it back and listen honestly, the way an examiner would hear it.
  4. Reveal the model answer, then work through the band-descriptor checklist after all four questions in the set.

Tips for Part 3 Answers

  • Give a clear opinion, then justify it with reasons, examples, or a wider perspective — Part 3 rewards developed ideas
  • Think beyond your own personal experience; examiners want to hear how you discuss issues and trends more generally
  • It's fine to disagree, compare two sides, or say something is "difficult to say" — as long as you explain why
  • Use discourse markers to structure longer answers ("On the one hand… on the other hand…", "I'd say the main reason is…")
  • Don't panic if a question feels abstract — pause briefly, then talk through your thinking out loud

Self-Assessment for This Discussion Set

Fluency & Coherence
  • I kept talking without long pauses, even on questions I found harder
  • My answers were logically organised, not just a list of disconnected points
  • I developed each answer well beyond a single sentence
Lexical Resource
  • I used less common or more abstract vocabulary appropriate to the topic
  • I paraphrased instead of repeating the examiner's exact wording
Grammatical Range & Accuracy
  • I used complex structures for hypothesising, comparing, or giving reasons (if, unless, although, whereas)
  • I used a mix of tenses accurately, including conditionals where relevant
Pronunciation
  • Listening back, my speech was clear and easy to understand
  • I used natural stress and intonation, rather than a flat tone
