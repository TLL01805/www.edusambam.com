Geometry: Angles, Polygons & Their Properties

Build geometry from first principles: angle facts, parallel lines, triangles, quadrilaterals and polygons, with the reason behind each rule, tiling and symmetry, and how to write exam reasons.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team|22 min read|Last updated: November 2026|Mathematics

Geometry is the mathematics of shape and space, and almost all of it rests on a handful of angle facts. Once you know that angles on a line add to 180°, that parallel lines make equal pairs of angles, and that the angles of a triangle add to 180°, you can work out the angles in polygons with any number of sides. This guide builds the whole chain step by step. For each fact it explains why it is true, gives worked examples, and shows how to write the reasons that examiners look for. It then moves on to quadrilaterals, polygons, tiling patterns and symmetry.

1.Types of Angle and How to Name Them

An angle measures a turn, in degrees. A full turn is 360°. We name angles by their size:

NameSize
AcuteBetween 0° and 90°
Right angleExactly 90°
ObtuseBetween 90° and 180°
StraightExactly 180°
ReflexBetween 180° and 360°

An angle can be named by a single letter at its corner, such as angle B, or by three letters with the corner in the middle: ∠ABC is the angle at B between the lines BA and BC. The middle letter is always the corner.

2.Three Basic Angle Facts

  • Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
  • Angles around a point add up to 360°.
  • Vertically opposite angles (the angles opposite each other where two straight lines cross) are equal.
Figure 1. Where two lines cross, the red angles are equal, the blue angles are equal, and each red plus blue angle on a line makes 180°.

Why vertically opposite angles are equal. Call two neighbouring angles x and y. They lie on a straight line, so x + y = 180°. The angle opposite x, call it z, also lies on a line with y, so z + y = 180°. Therefore z = x.

Example 1 · Lines and points

(a) Angles of 55°, 75° and x are on a straight line: 55 + 75 + x = 180, so x = 50°.

(b) Four angles at a point are 90°, 130°, 70° and y: y = 360 − (90 + 130 + 70) = 360 − 290 = 70°.

3.Parallel Lines and a Transversal

Parallel lines never meet and stay the same distance apart. They are marked with matching arrows. A line that crosses them is a transversal. It creates eight angles, and they can only have two different sizes: one acute value and its supplement (180° minus it).

Figure 2. A transversal crossing two parallel lines. Gold angles are all 72°, red angles are all 108°.
PairShapeRule
CorrespondingFEqual

Co-interior (allied)CAdd to 180°
Example 2 · Parallel line angles

One angle at the first crossing is 108°. The corresponding angle at the second crossing is 108°. The alternate angle (opposite side, between the lines) is 108°. The co-interior angle is 180 − 108 = 72°.

These rules work only if the lines are parallel. In a diagram, parallel lines are shown by arrows or stated in the question. Never assume that two lines that look parallel are parallel.

“The co-interior angles are 72° and 72°, because they are in matching positions.”

Which rule matches “same side, between the lines”?

The fix: co-interior angles are on the same side of the transversal, between the lines, and they add up to 180°. So they are 72° and 108°, not 72° and 72°. Equal pairs are corresponding (F) and alternate (Z). If you cannot remember, a quick sketch shows that one of the two angles must be acute and the other obtuse.

4.Triangles

The three angles inside any triangle add up to 180°. Triangles are named by their sides and by their angles.

By sidesBy angles
Scalene: no equal sidesAcute: all angles below 90°
Isosceles: two equal sidesRight-angled: one angle of 90°
Equilateral: three equal sidesObtuse: one angle above 90°
Figure 3. A proof that the angles of a triangle add up to 180°. The dashed line is parallel to the base.

Why the angles add up to 180°. Draw a line through the top corner parallel to the base, as in Figure 3. The angle x at the left of the top corner is alternate to the angle x at the base, so they are equal. In the same way the angle y on the right is equal to y at the base. The three angles x, z and y now lie along a straight line, so x + z + y = 180°. The proof is also found in Euclid’s Elements.

Example 3 · Third angle

Two angles of a triangle are 62° and 79°. The third is 180 − 62 − 79 = 39°. All three angles are different, so the triangle is scalene, and all three are below 90°, so it is acute.

The exterior angle theorem

If you extend one side of a triangle, the angle formed outside is an exterior angle. It equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles (the two not next to it). The reason: the exterior angle and the adjacent interior angle lie on a straight line, so they add to 180°. The three interior angles also add to 180°, so the exterior angle equals the other two.

Example 4 · Exterior angle

Interior angles at two corners are 48° and 65°. The exterior angle at the third corner is 48 + 65 = 113°. Check: the interior angle there is 180 − 113 = 67°, and 48 + 65 + 67 = 180.

Isosceles and equilateral triangles

In an isosceles triangle the two equal sides are opposite two equal angles, called the base angles. In an equilateral triangle all three angles are 60°. When two sides are marked equal on a diagram, mark the two angles opposite them as equal too.

Example 5 · Isosceles triangle

In an isosceles triangle the apex angle is 50°. Each base angle is (180 − 50) ÷ 2 = 65°. If the base is extended, the exterior angle at one base corner is 180 − 65 = 115°, which also equals 50° + 65° (the other two angles).

5.Quadrilaterals

A diagonal splits a quadrilateral into two triangles, so its four interior angles add up to 2 × 180° = 360°.

Example 6 · Missing angle

Three angles of a quadrilateral are 95°, 80° and 110°. The fourth is 360 − 95 − 80 − 110 = 75°.

QuadrilateralSidesAnglesDiagonals
ParallelogramOpposite sides equal and parallelOpposite angles equal; angles next to each other add to 180°Bisect each other

The square is a special case of both the rectangle and the rhombus, and every rectangle, rhombus and square is also a parallelogram. About the trapezium: in many syllabuses it means a quadrilateral with one pair of parallel sides (the same shape is called a trapezoid in the United States). Some courses allow two pairs, which would include parallelograms, so check the definition used by your own course.

Example 7 · A parallelogram with algebra

In parallelogram ABCD, angle A = (2x + 20)° and angle B = (3x − 10)°. Neighbouring angles add to 180°, so 5x + 10 = 180 and x = 34. Then A = 88° and B = 92°.

6.Polygons

A polygon is a closed shape with straight sides. A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal. Polygons are named by their number of sides: 3 triangle, 4 quadrilateral, 5 pentagon, 6 hexagon, 7 heptagon, 8 octagon, 9 nonagon, 10 decagon, 12 dodecagon.

Interior angles

From one corner of a polygon with n sides, you can draw diagonals to split it into (n − 2) triangles. Each triangle adds 180°, so the interior angles add up to (n − 2) × 180°.

Figure 4. A hexagon splits into 4 triangles, so its angles add to 4 × 180° = 720°.

Exterior angles

Extend each side of a convex polygon in turn. The turn you make at each corner is an exterior angle. Walking right round the polygon, you turn through one full circle, so the exterior angles always add up to 360°, whatever the number of sides.

Figure 5. In a regular pentagon each exterior angle is 360° ÷ 5 = 72°, so each interior angle is 180° − 72° = 108°.

The number of diagonals in a polygon with n sides is n(n − 3) ÷ 2. For a hexagon this is 6 × 3 ÷ 2 = 9.

Example 8 · Regular polygons

(a) A regular polygon has an exterior angle of 24°. n = 360 ÷ 24 = 15 sides. Each interior angle is 180 − 24 = 156°.

(b) Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 144°. The exterior angle is 180 − 144 = 36°, so n = 360 ÷ 36 = 10, a decagon.

(c) Could a regular polygon have interior angles of 100°? The exterior angle would be 80°, and 360 ÷ 80 = 4.5, which is not a whole number. No regular polygon has this angle.

Example 9 · An irregular polygon

(a) Four angles of a pentagon are 100°, 110°, 120° and 95°. The fifth is 540 − 425 = 115°.

(b) A hexagon has angles x, x, 2x, 2x, 150° and 150°. The sum is 720°, so 6x + 300 = 720 and x = 70°. The angles are 70°, 70°, 140°, 140°, 150°, 150°.

“The exterior angles of a decagon add up to 1440°, because that is its interior angle sum.”

Which of the two sums depends on n?

The fix: the interior angle sum grows with n: (n − 2) × 180°, which is 1440° for a decagon. The exterior angle sum is always 360°. For a regular decagon each exterior angle is 360° ÷ 10 = 36°.

7.Tiling Patterns (Tessellations)

A shape tessellates if copies of it fit together with no gaps or overlaps. At every meeting point the angles must add up to exactly 360°. A regular polygon can tile on its own only if its interior angle divides 360° exactly.

  • Equilateral triangle: 60° × 6 = 360°. It tiles.
  • Square: 90° × 4 = 360°. It tiles.
  • Regular hexagon: 120° × 3 = 360°. It tiles.
  • Regular pentagon: 360 ÷ 108 = 3.33…, which is not whole. It does not tile on its own.
  • Regular octagon: 360 ÷ 135 = 2.67…, so it does not tile alone. But two octagons and a square fit: 135° + 135° + 90° = 360°.

Any triangle and any quadrilateral can tile a plane, which is why floors and walls can use many different shapes.

8.Symmetry

A shape has line symmetry if a mirror line splits it into two halves that match exactly. It has rotational symmetry of order n if it fits onto itself n times in one full turn. Order 1 means no rotational symmetry.

ShapeLines of symmetryOrder of rotational symmetry

Rectangle (not square)22
Rhombus (not square)22
Parallelogram (no right angle)02
Kite11
Regular n-sided polygonnn
Example 10 · Regular pentagon

A regular pentagon has 5 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 5. It fits onto itself after every 360 ÷ 5 = 72° turn.

9.Writing Reasons in Exam Answers

In many geometry questions you must give a reason for each step, not just a number. Use the standard wording:

  • “Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.”
  • “Angles at a point add up to 360°.”
  • “Vertically opposite angles are equal.”
  • “Alternate angles are equal (parallel lines).”
  • “Corresponding angles are equal (parallel lines).”
  • “Co-interior angles add up to 180° (parallel lines).”
  • “Angles in a triangle add up to 180°.”
  • “The base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.”
  • “An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.”
  • “Angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°.”
Example 11 · A set-out answer

ABC is isosceles with AB = AC and angle BAC = 42°. BC is extended to D. Find angle ACD.

Angle ABC = angle ACB (base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal). Angle ABC = (180 − 42) ÷ 2 = 69° (angles in a triangle add up to 180°). Angle ACD = 42° + 69° = 111° (exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles).

Example 12 · Inside a regular hexagon

ABCDEF is a regular hexagon. Find angle ACD.

Each interior angle is 120°. Triangle ABC is isosceles (AB = BC) with angle B = 120°, so angle BCA = (180 − 120) ÷ 2 = 30°. Then angle ACD = angle BCD − angle BCA = 120° − 30° = 90°.

10.Where These Ideas Are Used

  • Tiling and flooring. Squares, triangles and hexagons are used for tiles because their angles fit round a point.
  • Honeycombs. Bees build cells that are roughly regular hexagons, which tile with no gaps. Mathematicians have shown that hexagons enclose a given area with the least total wall length. This is known as the honeycomb conjecture, proved by Thomas Hales in 1999.
  • Engineering. Triangles are rigid: a triangle of fixed sides cannot change its angles, which is why bridges, cranes and roof frames are built from triangles.
  • Design and maps. Parallel lines and angles are used in roads, circuit boards and surveying.

11.A Short History

The angle facts in this article were collected and proved in Euclid’s Elements, written in Greek around 300 BCE. The proof that the angles of a triangle add to 180° depends on Euclid’s fifth, or “parallel”, postulate. Over many centuries mathematicians asked whether that postulate could be proved from the others. In the 19th century they found that it cannot, and that consistent geometries exist in which it fails. These are the non-Euclidean geometries.

Did You Know?

On the curved surface of the Earth, the angles of a triangle can add up to more than 180°. Start at the North Pole, travel down one line of longitude to the equator, then along the equator by a quarter of the way round the world, and then back up another line of longitude to the pole. All three corners are right angles, so the angles add up to 270°. The 180° rule is a fact about flat surfaces.

12.Common Mistakes

  • Assuming lines are parallel because they look parallel. Look for arrows or a statement.
  • Mixing up alternate and co-interior angles. Z-shape angles are equal. C-shape angles add to 180°.
  • Using n instead of (n − 2) in the polygon formula.
  • Forgetting that the exterior angle sum is always 360°, not (n − 2) × 180°.
  • Measuring from a diagram. Diagrams are often not drawn to scale. Work out angles by reasoning, not with a protractor.
  • Stating an answer with no reason when the question says “give a reason”.
  • Calling a rhombus a square. A rhombus has equal sides but not necessarily right angles.
“Each interior angle of a regular octagon is 1080° ÷ 6, because the formula has (n − 2).”

Which number should the sum be shared between?

The fix: the (n − 2) is only used to find the total: (8 − 2) × 180° = 1080°. In a regular polygon the total is shared between all n angles, so each angle is 1080° ÷ 8 = 135°. Check using exterior angles: 360° ÷ 8 = 45°, and 180° − 45° = 135°.

“A rectangle has 4 lines of symmetry, including the two diagonals.”

Would folding along a diagonal make the two halves match?

The fix: fold a rectangle along a diagonal and the corners do not land on each other, because the sides are different lengths. A rectangle that is not a square has only 2 lines of symmetry: through the midpoints of opposite sides. Only a square (or a rhombus) has diagonals as lines of symmetry. For a rhombus, the diagonals are lines of symmetry, but the lines through the midpoints of opposite sides are not.

13.Try It Yourself

  1. A triangle has angles of 2x°, 3x° and 4x°. Find x and the size of the largest angle.
  2. Find the interior angle of a regular 18-sided polygon.
  3. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One of the angles is 65°. List the sizes of all eight angles.
Show answers

1. 2x + 3x + 4x = 180, so 9x = 180 and x = 20. The angles are 40°, 60° and 80°, and the largest is 80°.

2. Exterior angle = 360 ÷ 18 = 20°, so the interior angle is 180 − 20 = 160°.

3. Four angles are 65° and four are 180 − 65 = 115°. Each crossing has two of each, and the pairs match at the two crossings.

14.Exam-Style Questions

  1. [3 marks] The angles of a quadrilateral are 3x°, (2x + 10)°, (4x − 20)° and (x + 30)°. Find x and the largest angle.
  2. [3 marks] The exterior angle at one corner of a triangle is 115°. One of the interior opposite angles is 50°. Find the other, and say what type of triangle this is.
  3. [3 marks] An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 50°. Find a base angle, and the exterior angle at a base corner.
  4. [4 marks] Find the number of sides of a regular polygon with an interior angle of 156°, and the sum of its interior angles.
  5. [3 marks] The angles of a pentagon are 100°, 110°, 120°, 95° and x. Find x.
  6. [3 marks] In a parallelogram ABCD, angle A = (2x + 20)° and angle B = (3x − 10)°. Find x and the size of angle C.
  7. [4 marks] ABCDEF is a regular hexagon. (a) Find angle BAC. (b) Find angle ACD, giving a reason.
Show answers

1. 3x + 2x + 10 + 4x − 20 + x + 30 = 360, so 10x + 20 = 360 and x = 34. The angles are 102°, 78°, 116° and 64°. The largest is 116°.

2. The exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles: 115 = 50 + y, so the other is 65°. The third interior angle is 180 − 115 = 65°. Two angles are equal (65°, 65°), so the triangle is isosceles.

3. Base angle = (180 − 50) ÷ 2 = 65°. Exterior angle at a base corner = 180 − 65 = 115°.

4. Exterior angle = 180 − 156 = 24°, so n = 360 ÷ 24 = 15 sides. The interior angle sum = 13 × 180 = 2340°.

5. The angle sum of a pentagon is 540°. x = 540 − (100 + 110 + 120 + 95) = 540 − 425 = 115°.

6. Neighbouring angles add to 180°: 5x + 10 = 180, so x = 34. Angle A = 88°. Angle C is opposite A, so C = 88°.

7. (a) Each interior angle is 120° and triangle ABC is isosceles (AB = BC), so angle BAC = (180 − 120) ÷ 2 = 30°. (b) Angle ACD = 120° − 30° = 90°, because angle BCA = 30° (base angles of an isosceles triangle) and angle BCD = 120°.

15.Key Points

  • Angles on a line add to 180°, angles at a point to 360°, and vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • Parallel lines: corresponding (F) and alternate (Z) angles are equal, and co-interior (C) angles add to 180°.
  • Angles in a triangle add to 180°. An exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
  • Angles in a quadrilateral add to 360°.
  • A polygon with n sides has an interior angle sum of (n − 2) × 180°. Exterior angles always add to 360°.
  • In a regular polygon each exterior angle is 360° ÷ n and each interior angle is 180° minus that.
  • Regular triangles, squares and hexagons tile on their own because their angles divide 360°.
  • A regular n-sided polygon has n lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order n.
  • Always give a reason for each step, and never rely on a diagram being drawn to scale.

16.For Teachers and Parents

Geometry is learned best by doing and by explaining. Let learners tear the corners off a paper triangle and fit them together on a line to see 180° for themselves, and then ask them to prove it with the parallel line. For polygons, ask them to draw several polygons, split them into triangles from one corner, and look for the pattern in the number of triangles. The Polygon Calculator on this page lets learners test regular polygons and check whether an angle can belong to a regular polygon at all, and the Angle Rule Finder gives the exact wording of the reason for each rule. Emphasise why before how many degrees: learners who know the reason can rebuild a forgotten fact. Insist on written reasons, because that habit is what examiners reward.
