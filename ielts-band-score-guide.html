IELTS Band Score Guide: Understanding the 0–9 Scale

What each band actually means, how each of the four skills is scored differently, how your Overall Band Score is calculated and rounded, and what score you're likely to need — plus an interactive calculator to check your own average.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team · Reference guide + calculator + quiz

IELTS doesn't give a pass or fail — it places every candidate on a 9-point scale for each of the four skills, then combines them into a single Overall Band Score. Understanding exactly what that scale measures, and how it's calculated, makes it far easier to set a realistic target and track genuine progress toward it.

1.The Band Scale at a Glance

2.How Listening and Reading Are Scored

Listening and Reading are both marked out of 40 raw points (one point per correct answer), which is then converted into a band score using a conversion table. The exact conversion shifts slightly from test to test to keep difficulty fair across different versions, but the table below illustrates roughly how it tends to work for Academic Listening and Reading.

3.How Writing and Speaking Are Scored

Writing and Speaking can't be marked by counting correct answers — instead, trained examiners assess each one against four separate criteria, then average those four scores to get the skill's band. Explore how typical performance changes across bands 5 to 8, the range most candidates are aiming for.

4.Calculating Your Overall Band Score

Your Overall Band Score is the average of your four individual skill scores, rounded using a specific rule: averages ending in .25 round up to the nearest half band, and averages ending in .75 round up to the next whole band. Try it yourself below.

5.What Band Score Do You Actually Need?

PurposeTypical Range Often Requested*
Foundation / pathway programs4.5 – 5.5
Undergraduate degree6.0 – 6.5
Postgraduate degree6.5 – 7.5
Competitive / specialist programs7.0+
Skilled migration (points-based systems)6.0 – 8.0+, depending on visa category and points sought
Teaching, nursing & healthcare registration7.0 – 7.5, often with high per-skill minimums
*These ranges are general and illustrative only. Always confirm the exact overall and per-skill requirements directly with the specific institution, employer, or immigration authority, since requirements vary and change over time.

6.Tips for Moving Up a Band

Practical Strategy
  • Target your weakest skill first — since the overall score is an average, that's usually where the fastest gains are available
  • Study the actual band descriptors for your target band, not just general English improvement, so you know exactly what examiners are looking for
  • Get consistent, honest feedback — self-assessment against real criteria, a tutor, or a study partner — rather than guessing at your level
  • Practise regularly under real timed conditions, not just untimed study, since time pressure changes performance significantly
  • Review your error patterns across practice attempts instead of simply doing more tests without reflection
