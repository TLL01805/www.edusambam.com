Sports of the World: Rules, Terms & Records

Cricket, football, hockey, and the Olympics — the terms, records, and formats that show up constantly in GK tests and everyday conversation alike.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team | 22 min read | General Knowledge |Last updated: October 2026

Sports questions appear in general knowledge tests because they combine memorable numbers (records, dates, scores) with rules that are simple to test but easy to get subtly wrong. This article covers the sports most relevant to readers in Pakistan and South Asia, cricket and hockey, then football, the Olympic Games and other well-known sports. It includes the latest major results through 2026, worked examples you can calculate yourself, and a section that corrects common sporting myths.

In this article

How sport is organised · cricket · hockey · football · the Olympic Games · other sports · records · myths corrected · four worked examples · key terms · practice questions and a quiz.

1.How World Sport Is Organised

Every major sport has a governing body that writes the rules, organises competitions and keeps records. Knowing the names helps in quizzes and in following the news.

Sport or eventGoverning body
CricketInternational Cricket Council (ICC); in Pakistan, the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB)
FootballFIFA (founded 1904)
Field hockeyInternational Hockey Federation (FIH)
Olympic GamesInternational Olympic Committee (IOC, founded 1894)
TennisInternational Tennis Federation (ITF), with the four Grand Slam tournaments run separately

2.Cricket

Cricket is a bat-and-ball game played between two teams of 11. One team bats and tries to score runs; the other bowls and fields and tries to dismiss the batters. The action happens on a rectangular strip called the pitch, 22 yards (about 20.12 m) long, in the middle of an oval field.

Figure 1. A cricket field seen from above, with common fielding positions for a right-handed batter. The red ball travels down the pitch from the bowler.

Formats

FormatLength
Test matchUp to 5 days, each side bats up to two innings, no limit on overs
One Day International (ODI)50 overs per side
Twenty20 (T20)20 overs per side

An over is six legal balls bowled by one bowler.

Scoring and getting out

  • Runs: batters score by running between the wickets, or by hitting the ball to the boundary for four runs (along the ground) or six (over the rope without bouncing).
  • Extras: runs added without a hit, such as wides, no-balls, byes and leg byes.
  • Ways of getting out: bowled, caught, LBW, run out, stumped and hit wicket.

Key terms

TermMeaning
LBWLeg Before Wicket: the batter is out if the ball would have hit the stumps but hit their leg (or body) first, under certain conditions
DuckA batter is dismissed without scoring
CenturyA batter scores 100 or more runs in an innings
Maiden overAn over in which the bowler concedes no runs
YorkerA ball bowled to land at the batter's feet, hard to hit
GooglyA deceptive spin delivery that turns the opposite way to how it looks
DLS methodDuckworth-Lewis-Stern method: a formula used to set a fair target when rain interrupts a match
Worked example 1: Batting average and strike rate

Question: A batter scores 450 runs in 10 innings, was not out twice, and faced 500 balls. Find the batting average and the strike rate.

Step 1. Batting average = runs ÷ times dismissed. Times dismissed = 10 − 2 = 8.

Step 2. Average = 450 ÷ 8 = 56.25.

Step 3. Strike rate = (runs ÷ balls faced) × 100 = (450 ÷ 500) × 100 = 90.

Answer: An average of 56.25 and a strike rate of 90 runs per 100 balls.

Worked example 2: Economy and required run rate

Question: (a) A bowler bowls 4 overs and concedes 28 runs. What is the economy rate? (b) A team chases 280 in 50 overs and is 150 for 3 after 30 overs. What run rate does it now need?

Step 1. Economy rate = runs conceded ÷ overs bowled = 28 ÷ 4 = 7.0.

Step 2. Runs still needed = 280 − 150 = 130. Overs left = 50 − 30 = 20.

Step 3. Required rate = 130 ÷ 20 = 6.5.

Answer: (a) 7 runs per over. (b) 6.5 runs per over.

Pakistan and cricket

Pakistan has one of the most remarkable records in world cricket. Under Imran Khan the team won the 1992 Cricket World Cup in Australia, beating England in the final at Melbourne. It won the ICC World Twenty20 in 2009 and the ICC Champions Trophy in 2017. The Pakistan Super League (PSL), the country's T20 franchise league, began in 2016. A record often quoted for Pakistan is the fastest recorded delivery in cricket history, 161.3 km/h by Shoaib Akhtar in 2003.

Other records and recent results

  • Highest individual Test score: 400 not out by Brian Lara of the West Indies (2004).
  • Most Test wickets: 800 by Muttiah Muralitharan of Sri Lanka. Shane Warne of Australia took 708.
  • Highest individual ODI score: 264 by Rohit Sharma of India (2014).
  • India won the ICC Men's T20 World Cup in 2007, 2024 and 2026, the first team to win it three times. In the 2026 final, held on 8 March in Ahmedabad, India beat New Zealand by 96 runs.

3.Field Hockey

Field hockey is played by two teams of 11 on a pitch about 91.4 m long and 55 m wide. Players hit the ball with the flat side of a curved stick, and goals can be scored only from inside the shooting circle. Matches are played in four 15-minute quarters. Penalty corners, penalty strokes and green, yellow and red cards are part of the game. Since the 1970s, top-level hockey has been played on artificial turf rather than grass, which made the game faster and changed which countries dominated.

Pakistan and hockey. Field hockey is recognised as Pakistan's national sport, though cricket is by far the most popular. Pakistan has won three Olympic gold medals in hockey (1960, 1968 and 1984) and four Hockey World Cups (1971, 1978, 1982 and 1994). This was long a record on its own. In 2026, Germany won its fourth title (after 2002, 2006 and 2023) by beating Spain 1-0 in the final in Belgium, so Pakistan and Germany now share the record. India holds the record for Olympic hockey, with eight gold medals.

4.Football

Football (soccer) is played by two teams of 11 on a rectangular pitch, typically about 105 m by 68 m. A normal match has two halves of 45 minutes. The goalkeeper is the only player allowed to handle the ball inside their own penalty area.

Figure 2. A football pitch with its main markings. The ball passes up the pitch in the animation.
TermMeaning
OffsideAn attacker is in an offside position if, when the ball is played to them, they are nearer to the opponents' goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent (usually the last outfield defender)
Hat-trickOne player scores three goals in a match
Extra timeAdditional periods played if a knockout match is level
Penalty kickA free shot at goal from the penalty spot, 11 m (12 yards) from the goal line
VARVideo Assistant Referee: reviews key decisions using video replay (first used at the World Cup in 2018)

The FIFA World Cup is held every four years. The first was in 1930, in Uruguay, and Uruguay won it. In July 2026, the first World Cup with 48 teams, hosted jointly by the United States, Canada and Mexico, was won by Spain, who beat Argentina 1-0 after extra time in the final. It was Spain's second title, after 2010.

5.The Olympic Games

The Ancient Olympics began in 776 BC in Olympia, Greece, according to tradition, and continued for roughly 1,200 years before being discontinued late in the 4th century AD. The Modern Olympics were revived in 1896 in Athens, mainly through the efforts of the French educator Pierre de Coubertin, and the International Olympic Committee had been founded in 1894. The Olympic motto is Citius, Altius, Fortius – Communiter: "Faster, Higher, Stronger – Together".

The Summer Games are held every four years. The first Winter Games were held in 1924, and since 1994 the Winter Games have taken place two years away from the Summer Games. The Paralympic Games, for athletes with disabilities, began in Rome in 1960.

Figure 4. The Olympic cycle: Summer and Winter Games alternate every two years. The marker moves along the timeline.
Worked example 3: Counting Olympiads

Question: The first modern Olympic Games were held in 1896. Paris 2024 is officially the Games of the XXXIII Olympiad (33rd). How does that work?

Step 1. An Olympiad is a four-year period. Count from 1896 to 2024: 2024 − 1896 = 128 years.

Step 2. 128 ÷ 4 = 32 four-year periods have passed, so Paris starts the 33rd Olympiad (32 + 1).

Step 3. Check: Los Angeles 2028 is the 34th.

Answer: 33. The number counts the four-year periods, even when Games were cancelled in 1916, 1940 and 1944 because of war.

Pakistan at the Olympics. Besides the three hockey golds, Arshad Nadeem won the javelin gold medal at Paris 2024 with a throw of 92.97 m, an Olympic record. It was Pakistan's first individual Olympic gold medal and its first gold of any kind since 1984.

Fast Fact

The five interlocking Olympic rings stand for the union of five inhabited regions of the world (Africa, the Americas, Asia, Europe and Oceania), which is why there are five rings and not seven, the number of continents often taught.

Worked example 4: How fast is a 9.58-second 100 m?

Question: Usain Bolt ran 100 m in 9.58 s. What was his average speed in m/s and in km/h?

Step 1. Speed = distance ÷ time = 100 ÷ 9.58 ≈ 10.44 m/s.

Step 2. To convert to km/h, multiply by 3.6: 10.44 × 3.6 ≈ 37.6 km/h.

Answer: About 10.4 m/s, or about 37.6 km/h. (His top speed during the race was higher than his average.)

8.Myths Corrected

"'Hat-trick' was invented for football, for scoring three goals"

Many assume this term originated in football simply because it is so common there today.

The fix: The term "hat-trick" originated in cricket, describing a bowler taking three wickets with three consecutive balls, a feat for which, according to the traditional account, a bowler in 1858 was presented with a hat. It was later adopted by football and other sports to mean three achievements, usually goals, by one player.

"'Love,' meaning zero in tennis, must just be a random word choice"

Students often assume tennis scoring terms are arbitrary.

The fix: "Love" meaning zero is widely believed to come from the French word l'oeuf ("the egg"), because a zero looks like an egg. The exact origin is debated among historians, and this is the most commonly cited explanation.

"A player is offside if they are ahead of the ball"

This is the most common way fans describe the rule.

The fix: The rule compares the attacker's position with the second-last opponent and the ball when the ball is played to them. A player in their own half, or level with the second-last defender, is not offside, and a player cannot be offside from a throw-in, goal kick or corner kick.

"Cricket is Pakistan's national sport"

Cricket is the most popular sport in Pakistan by far, which is why many people assume this.

The fix: Field hockey is generally recognised as Pakistan's national sport. Popularity and official status are not the same thing.

Real-World Example

When Pakistan or India play a cricket match, terms like "century", "maiden over" and "DLS method" appear constantly in live commentary. Knowing them turns watching from confusing to genuinely engaging, and a quick calculation of the required run rate lets you follow the match like a commentator.

Did You Know?

Pakistan's 1992 World Cup-winning captain, Imran Khan, later served as Prime Minister of Pakistan from 2018 to 2022, one of the rare people to have captained a World Cup-winning team and then led their country's government.

10.Summary

  • Cricket has three main formats: Test (up to 5 days), ODI (50 overs) and T20 (20 overs).
  • Pakistan won the Cricket World Cup in 1992, the T20 World Cup in 2009 and the Champions Trophy in 2017.
  • Pakistan won three Olympic hockey golds and four Hockey World Cups; Germany matched that record in 2026.
  • Spain won the 2026 FIFA World Cup, the first with 48 teams; Brazil still holds the record with five titles.
  • The modern Olympics began in 1896; Arshad Nadeem won Pakistan's first individual Olympic gold in 2024.
  • "Hat-trick" came from cricket, and the offside rule is about the second-last opponent, not just "ahead of the ball".

11.Check Your Understanding

1. How many balls are in an over, and how many overs does each side get in an ODI?

Six balls per over; 50 overs per side.

2. A bowler concedes 36 runs in 6 overs. What is the economy rate?

36 ÷ 6 = 6.0 runs per over.

3. Which two countries share the record of four Men's Hockey World Cup titles?

Pakistan and Germany.

4. Who won the 2026 FIFA World Cup, and who was the runner-up?

Spain won, beating Argentina 1-0 after extra time.

5. Which sport did the term "hat-trick" originate in?

Cricket.

6. What is special about Arshad Nadeem's 2024 Olympic gold?

It was Pakistan's first individual Olympic gold medal, and he set an Olympic record of 92.97 m in the javelin.

For Teachers and Parents

Suggested activities: have students work out batting averages and strike rates from a real scorecard; mark out a to-scale football pitch or a 22-yard cricket pitch in the playground; hold a class quiz on records. Discussion prompts: Why do rules need governing bodies? What makes a record "great"? Sports results change, and records are broken, so the article gives results through 2026 and says when a record is shared; always check the latest figures from an official source for any competition.
