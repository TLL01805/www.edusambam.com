Section A — Multiple Choice

30 marks

15 questions · 2 marks each. Choose the best answer.

1.The first line of defence against many pathogens includes:Basic2 pts
Why: The correct answer is B. Skin, mucous membranes, cilia and secretions such as tears and stomach acid block or remove many pathogens before any immune cells are needed.
2.Innate immunity is best described as:Basic2 pts
Why: The correct answer is D. Innate defences are always ready and act quickly against many kinds of pathogen. They do not need earlier exposure.
3.Antibodies are produced by:Basic2 pts
Why: The correct answer is A. When a B cell is activated it can become a plasma cell, which secretes large amounts of antibody.
4.A key feature of adaptive immunity is:Basic2 pts
Why: The correct answer is C. Adaptive immunity recognises particular antigens through specific receptors and keeps memory cells for faster responses later.
5.Vaccination works primarily by:Basic2 pts
Why: The correct answer is B. A vaccine shows the immune system a harmless form or part of a pathogen so that memory is formed before real infection.
6.A booster dose can be useful because:Intermediate2 pts
Why: The correct answer is D. A repeat exposure reminds the immune system, strengthens memory and can raise antibody levels that have fallen over time.
7.A person infected with a virus is not necessarily helped by an antibiotic because:Intermediate2 pts
Why: The correct answer is A. Antibiotics act on features of bacteria, such as cell walls or bacterial enzymes. Viruses replicate inside host cells and lack those targets.
8.Antimicrobial resistance is strongly promoted when:Intermediate2 pts
Why: The correct answer is C. Drug exposure selects for variants that can survive. Those variants reproduce and pass resistance on, so resistance becomes more common.
9.Autoimmune disease involves:Intermediate2 pts
Why: The correct answer is B. In autoimmune disease the immune system wrongly treats the body's own molecules as targets and damages healthy tissue.
10.An allergy is best understood as:Intermediate2 pts
Why: The correct answer is D. In allergy the immune system overreacts to a harmless substance such as pollen or certain foods.
11.Which immune cell type can directly kill infected or abnormal target cells?Advanced2 pts
Why: The correct answer is C. Cytotoxic T cells recognise infected or abnormal cells and trigger them to die. Red blood cells carry oxygen, platelets help clotting and osteocytes are bone cells.
12.Inflammation can be useful because it:Advanced2 pts
Why: The correct answer is A. Inflammation increases blood flow and brings immune cells and molecules to the site of injury or infection. Excessive or long-lasting inflammation can be harmful.
13.Why can a second exposure to the same antigen sometimes produce a faster response?Advanced2 pts
Why: The correct answer is D. Memory B and T cells made during the first response recognise the antigen quickly and multiply faster.
14.A vaccine with high effectiveness can reduce disease spread because:Advanced2 pts
Why: The correct answer is B. When fewer people can be infected, a pathogen has fewer chances to pass from person to person. No vaccine is perfect for everyone.
15.Which statement best integrates immunity and public health?Advanced2 pts
Why: The correct answer is C. Protection in individuals changes how an infection spreads, and how it spreads in turn affects who is at risk.

Section B — True / False

20 marks

10 questions · 2 marks each.

16.The immune system can distinguish many molecular patterns using specialised receptors.Basic2 pts
Why: The answer is True. Immune cells carry receptors that recognise many different molecular patterns, including antigens.
17.Innate immunity is completely absent until after vaccination.Basic2 pts
Why: The answer is False. Innate defences are present from birth and do not depend on vaccination.
18.Antibodies are proteins that can bind specific targets.Basic2 pts
Why: The answer is True. Each antibody has binding sites that fit a particular antigen.
19.All inflammation is harmful.Basic2 pts
Why: The answer is False. Short-term inflammation helps defence and repair. Only excessive or prolonged inflammation causes harm.
20.Vaccines are designed to induce immune memory against particular targets.Basic2 pts
Why: The answer is True. Vaccines present antigens so that memory cells are formed before real infection.
21.Antibiotics are effective treatments for every viral infection.Intermediate2 pts
Why: The answer is False. Antibiotics act on bacteria, not viruses.
22.Antimicrobial resistance can spread through microbial populations.Intermediate2 pts
Why: The answer is True. Resistant microbes reproduce, and resistance genes can also pass between microbes.
23.Autoimmune reactions can involve immune recognition of self molecules as targets.Advanced2 pts
Why: The answer is True. This is the key idea of autoimmunity: the immune system targets the body's own molecules.
24.A person with immune memory can never become infected again.Advanced2 pts
Why: The answer is False. Memory lowers risk but does not guarantee complete protection. Some pathogens change, and immunity can fade.
25.Herd or community protection can depend on how many people are susceptible and how efficiently an infection spreads.Advanced2 pts
Why: The answer is True. Both the share of people who can be infected and how easily the pathogen spreads affect community protection.

Section C — Fill in the Blank

20 marks

10 questions · 2 marks each. Type the missing word or term.

26.The first line of physical defence includes the skin and ______ membranes.Intermediate2 pts
Why: Mucous membranes line openings such as the nose and throat and trap pathogens in mucus.
27.The branch of immunity that includes rapid, broad defences is ______ immunity.Intermediate2 pts
Why: Innate immunity acts quickly against many kinds of pathogen.
28.Cells that secrete large amounts of antibodies are called ______ cells.Intermediate2 pts
Why: Plasma cells are activated B cells that make antibodies.
29.Long-lived cells that contribute to faster secondary responses are ______ cells.Intermediate2 pts
Why: Memory cells remain after an infection and respond fast to the same antigen.
30.A substance or molecular structure recognised by specific immune receptors is an ______.Intermediate2 pts
Why: An antigen is what immune receptors and antibodies recognise.
31.A preparation that trains the immune system to recognise a pathogen without causing the disease is called a ______.Advanced2 pts
Why: A vaccine primes the immune system and builds memory.
32.The inappropriate immune response to harmless substances seen in hay fever is an ______.Advanced2 pts
Why: Hay fever is an allergy to harmless substances such as pollen.
33.Immune attack against the body's own tissues is characteristic of ______ disease.Advanced2 pts
Why: Autoimmune disease involves immune attack on the body's own tissues.
34.The ability of microbes to survive drugs that once killed them is called antimicrobial ______.Advanced2 pts
Why: This is antimicrobial resistance (AMR).
35.The movement of an infection through a population depends partly on transmission and the number of ______ people.Advanced2 pts
Why: Susceptible people can still be infected, so their number affects spread.
