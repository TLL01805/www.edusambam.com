Writing Practice & Self-Assessment: Task 1 (Academic — Data Description)

Format: IELTS Academic Task 1 — describing a bar chart, line graph, pie chart, table, or process diagram

Choose a chart, table, or diagram and write a 150-word summary describing its key features, just like the real IELTS Academic Task 1. There's no auto-scoring here — instead, you'll compare your own writing against a model answer and work through the same criteria an examiner would use.

How this helps your writing

  1. Choose a task below — a bar chart, line graph, pair of pie charts, table, or process diagram.
  2. Pick Practice Mode (structure checklist visible, no time pressure) while you're learning the format, or Exam Mode (checklist hidden, strict 20-minute countdown) to test yourself properly.
  3. Write at least 150 words summarising the key features and making relevant comparisons — the word counter tracks your progress live.
  4. When you're done, reveal the model answer and work through the band-descriptor checklist yourself, comparing your response against each criterion honestly.

Useful Language for Describing Data

Describing Change
  • rose / increased / climbed sharply
  • fell / declined / dropped gradually
  • remained stable / stayed constant
  • fluctuated slightly
Comparing & Contrasting
  • in contrast / whereas / while
  • similarly / likewise
  • considerably higher/lower than
  • the same cannot be said for…
Overview Phrases
  • overall, it is clear that…
  • the most striking feature is…
  • a notable exception is…
Process Diagrams Only
  • the process begins with / starts when…
  • following this / after this stage…
  • in the final stage…
  • use passive voice: "the bottles are collected"

    Self-Assessment

    Task Achievement
    • I paraphrased the question in my own words in sentence 1
    • I included a clear overview of the 2 main overall features (no specific numbers)
    • I covered the key data points without listing every single number
    • I made relevant comparisons between data points, where appropriate
    • I avoided giving opinions or reasons why the data is the way it is
    • I wrote at least 150 words
    Coherence & Cohesion
    • My answer is organised into clear paragraphs (intro, overview, 2 body paragraphs)
    • I used linking words naturally, without overusing the same one repeatedly
    • Each paragraph focuses on one clear idea or group of data
    Lexical Resource
    • I used a range of vocabulary for describing trends (not just "increased" every time)
    • I used precise, topic-appropriate vocabulary rather than vague words like "a lot"
    • I avoided repeating the same word or phrase too often
    Grammatical Range & Accuracy
    • I used a mix of simple and complex sentence structures
    • I used the correct tense consistently for the data shown (e.g. past tense for historical figures)
    • I checked for basic errors in spelling, articles, and subject-verb agreement
