Commonly Misspelled Words in English: Lists, Rules and Memory Tricks

Accommodate, necessary, definitely, separate. Learn the spelling patterns, rules and memory tricks behind the words that cause the most mistakes.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team | 24 min read | Last updated: November 2026 | Vocabulary

Even well-read students lose marks to spelling. Words such as accommodate, necessary, definitely and separate are misspelled by native speakers and learners alike. The good news is that most mistakes fall into a small number of patterns, and once you know the patterns, you can fix whole groups of words at a time.

This guide explains the main causes of spelling mistakes and gives 233 commonly misspelled words in nine groups, each with the common wrong spelling and a hint about where the mistake happens. It also covers ten spelling rules, British and American spelling differences, memory tricks, key points, ten practice questions and a teacher activity.

It connects with Commonly Confused Words, Singular and Plural Nouns and Common Errors in English. Spelling in this guide follows British English.

1.Why Do We Misspell Words?

English spelling is difficult because it has borrowed words from Latin, Greek, French, German and many other languages, and the spelling often preserves the history of the word, not its sound. Common causes of mistakes are:

  • Double letters. It is hard to hear whether a letter is doubled: occurrence, embarrass.
  • Silent letters. Some letters are written but not pronounced: debt, knowledge, autumn.
  • Vowels in unstressed syllables. The vowel sounds the same in -ant and -ent, so we guess: dependent, independence.
  • Foreign spellings. Restaurant, entrepreneur and rhythm keep their French or Greek spellings.
  • Similar-looking patterns. -able / -ible, -ary / -ery and ie / ei cause constant confusion.

2.Ten Spelling Rules That Help

RuleExamples
i before e, except after cbelieve, achieve, receive, ceiling; exceptions: weird, seize, height, leisure
Drop the silent e before a vowel suffixhope → hoping, make → making; keep it before a consonant: hope → hopeful
Keep the e after soft c or g before -able, -ousnotice → noticeable, courage → courageous
Double the final consonant (one vowel + one consonant, stressed syllable)run → running, begin → beginning, refer → referred
Change y to i before a suffixhappy → happiness, carry → carried; keep y before -ing: carrying
Full + suffix: one lcareful, helpful, beautiful, grateful
All + word: one lalways, already, almost, also, although, altogether
Prefix + word: keep both lettersun + necessary → unnecessary, dis + satisfied → dissatisfied, mis + spell → misspell
Add -ly to a word ending in l: keep bothreal → really, final → finally, accidental → accidentally
Words ending in -ic add -allybasic → basically, specific → specifically (exception: publicly)

Rules have exceptions, but they work for most words. For the exceptions, use the lists below.

14.How to Improve Your Spelling

  • Look, say, cover, write, check. Look at the word, say it in syllables, cover it, write it from memory and check.
  • Keep a personal list of the words you misspell and revise it every week.
  • Break words into syllables and say each part: nec-es-sar-y.
  • Learn word families. If you can spell sign, you can spell signature and design.
  • Read widely. Seeing words in print builds your visual memory.
  • Proofread slowly, reading from the end to the beginning, so that you see each word on its own.
  • Do not trust spell-check completely. It cannot tell their from there.
Key points to remember
  • Most spelling mistakes come from double letters, silent letters, unstressed vowels and foreign spellings.
  • Learn the rules: i before e except after c, drop silent e, double the final consonant.
  • Learn words in groups with the same ending: -able/-ible, -ance/-ence, -ary/-ery.
  • Only three words end in -ceed (exceed, proceed, succeed) and only one ends in -sede (supersede).
  • Keep one style, British or American, within a piece of writing.
  • Use memory tricks and syllables for the hardest words.
For teachers and parents

Activity: give each student a list of ten words from one group in the tables, ask them to use look, say, cover, write and check, and then give a dictation test the next day. A group dictation game, in which teams race to write the correct spelling on the board, also works well. Students who like these tests may also enjoy the spelling bee practice tests on this website.

Please note: this guide uses British spelling. Some words have more than one accepted spelling, and only the most common correct forms are shown.
