Gender in English: Masculine, Feminine, Common and Neuter Nouns

Actor and actress, hero and heroine, bull and cow. Learn the four genders, every way of forming the feminine, the hardest pairs to remember, and modern gender-neutral words.

Why do we say actor and actress but teacher for both? Why is a ship called she? Why is a bachelor matched with a spinster, a widower with a widow, a wizard with a witch? Gender in English follows its own logic, and this guide explains every type of gender, every pattern for forming the feminine, and the tricky pairs that are hard to remember.

This guide explains the four genders of nouns, the pronouns that match them, the three ways of forming the feminine, and the modern use of gender-neutral words. It includes tables with over 200 words, key points, common mistakes, ten practice questions and a teacher activity.

It builds on Parts of Speech and connects with Singular and Plural Nouns and Common Errors in English. Spelling in this guide follows British English.

1.What Is Gender in Grammar?

In grammar, gender is the classification of nouns (and the pronouns that replace them) as masculine, feminine, common or neuter. In many languages, such as French, Spanish, German and Urdu, every noun has a grammatical gender, even a table or a book, and adjectives or verbs may change to match it.

English is different. It uses natural gender: a noun is masculine or feminine only when it names a male or female person or animal. Things and ideas are neuter. This is why table needs no gender but lion and lioness do.

2.The Four Genders

GenderMeaningPronounsExamples
Masculinemale persons and animalshe, him, hisman, boy, king, uncle, lion, bull
Femininefemale persons and animalsshe, her, herswoman, girl, queen, aunt, lioness, cow
Commoneither male or female (the sex is not shown by the word)he or she, theyteacher, student, doctor, cousin, friend, parent, child
Neuterthings, ideas, qualities and animals when sex is unknown or unimportantit, itstable, river, freedom, honesty, the baby (often), a bird

Common gender is sometimes called dual gender or, in modern grammar, gender-neutral. Some grammars also use the term epicene for words like sheep and mouse, which have one form for both sexes.

3.Gender and Pronouns

  • Masculine: The king lost his crown. He was angry.
  • Feminine: The queen found her crown. She was relieved.
  • Neuter: The crown was heavy. It was made of gold.
  • Common gender: when the person is unknown, writers once used he. Modern usage prefers he or she, a rewrite in the plural, or the singular they: Each student must bring their own book. Singular they has been used in English for centuries and is now accepted by major dictionaries and style guides.
  • Babies and animals are often called it when the sex is unknown, but he or she when known or when the speaker feels close to the animal (my dog… she).

4.Three Ways of Forming the Feminine

MethodHow it worksExamples
A different wordThe masculine and feminine are unrelated wordsfather / mother, king / queen, bull / cow, bachelor / spinster
Adding or changing a suffix-ess, -ine, -trix, -a, -ette, -enne, -e (some ending in -or or -er)actor / actress, hero / heroine, executor / executrix, sultan / sultana, usher / usherette
Adding a gender wordA word such as he, she, boy, girl, man, woman, male, female is addedhe-goat / she-goat, boy friend / girl friend, landlord / landlady, woman doctor

6.Adding -ess

9.Common Gender Nouns

These nouns do not show whether the person is male or female. The sex is shown by context or by a gender word: a male teacher, a female doctor.

teacher, student, doctor, cousin, friend, parent, child, baby, pupil, orphan, speaker, person, guest, neighbour, cook, singer, artist, driver, pilot, worker, minister, president, sibling, relative, servant, enemy, musician, dancer, lawyer, judge, engineer, scientist, citizen, colleague, customer, patient, passenger, teenager, infant, adult, spouse, athlete.

10.Neuter Nouns

Neuter nouns name things without sex: objects, places, qualities and ideas. They are replaced by it. Examples: table, book, river, mountain, chair, house, city, country, car, pen, computer, idea, freedom, happiness, honesty, justice, time, school, bridge, cloud, desk, window, lamp, ocean, music.

11.Personification: Giving Gender to Things

Writers sometimes give a thing a gender to make it feel alive. This is called personification.

ThingUsual genderExample
shipfeminine, by sailors' traditionThe ship lost her mast in the storm.
countryfeminine (older style)France is proud of her history.
moon, earth, naturefeminineMother Nature protects her children.
sun, time, death, warmasculineFather Time waits for no one.
boats, cars, enginesfeminine, by affectionate habitShe is a fine old car.

Using it for a ship or a country is now standard and fully correct.

13.Common Mistakes with Gender

MistakeCorrectWhy
doctoress, teacheress, singeressdoctor, teacher, singerThese nouns are common gender and need no feminine form.
The lioness and his cubsThe lioness and her cubsThe pronoun must agree with the gender.
She is my brother.She is my sister.Use the correct feminine noun.
The baby lost her toy (when sex is unknown)The baby lost its toy or their toyUse its or their when the sex is not known.
A widower womanA widowWidower is already masculine.
Mary is an authoress and a poetess.Mary is an author and a poet.Plain words are preferred for both sexes in modern writing.
a female actressan actressActress already means female.
Everyone must bring his book (for mixed groups)Everyone must bring their bookUse their or rewrite the sentence.
Key points to remember
  • English has four genders: masculine, feminine, common and neuter.
  • English uses natural gender, so only living beings have a masculine or feminine form.
  • The feminine is formed by a different word, a suffix such as -ess, -ine or -trix, or an added gender word.
  • Common gender nouns, such as teacher and doctor, do not show sex.
  • Things and ideas are neuter, but writers may personify them.
  • Modern usage prefers gender-neutral words: chairperson, firefighter, police officer, flight attendant.
  • Do not use a double feminine, such as a female actress.
For teachers and parents

Activity: give students cards with masculine words (wizard, heir, stallion, bachelor, sultan) and ask them to write the feminine forms, then to say which method was used (different word, suffix or added word). Follow with a rewriting task: students change five gendered job titles to neutral forms and use each in a sentence.

Please note: some of the forms in this guide are old-fashioned, and usage keeps changing. Students should follow their syllabus, but should understand both traditional and modern forms.
