Units of Measurement: Length, Mass, Capacity, Time & Area

Every measurement compares a quantity to a standard unit. Learn the ladder of metric units and the exact pattern for converting length, mass, capacity, time, area and speed.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team|14 min read|Last updated: October 2026|Mathematics

A tailor cuts cloth in metres and centimetres. A pharmacist weighs in grams and milligrams. A farmer buys water by the litre and measures land by the hectare. All of them depend on one idea: a measurement is only useful when everyone uses the same units, and when you can change from one unit to another without making a mistake. This article gives you the exact pattern for doing that for length, mass, capacity, time, area and volume.

1.What Is Measurement?

Measurement means comparing a quantity to an agreed, standard unit. Saying “the table is 2 metres long” only means something because everyone agrees on exactly how long a metre is. Long ago, people measured with body parts such as the hand, the foot and the cubit (the length of the forearm). The problem was obvious: your foot is not the same size as mine. Standard units solved that problem, so that a shopkeeper in Karachi and an engineer in Lahore mean the same thing by “one kilogram”.

Every measurement has two parts: a number and a unit. “5” alone means nothing, but “5 cm” and “5 km” are very different. Always write the unit.

2.The Metric System

Most of the world uses the metric system, which is built entirely around powers of 10. Every unit shares the same small set of prefixes, so learning them once unlocks length, mass and capacity at the same time.

PrefixMeaningMultiplier
kilo-one thousand×1,000
(base unit)metre, gram, litre×1
centi-one hundredth×0.01
milli-one thousandth×0.001

So a kilometre is 1,000 metres, a millilitre is one thousandth of a litre, and a centimetre is one hundredth of a metre. (Centi- means “hundred”, like the word century, and milli- means “thousand”, like the word millennium.) For a complete list of official prefixes, including very large and very small ones, see the link in Further Reading.

3.Units of Length

UnitSymbolEqual To
Millimetremm0.1 cm
Centimetrecm10 mm
Metrem100 cm = 1,000 mm
Kilometrekm1,000 m

It helps to know a few real sizes so you can judge whether an answer is sensible. A fingernail is about 1 cm wide. A door is about 2 m high. A cricket pitch is 22 yards long, which is about 20.12 m. A steady walk covers about 1 km in 10 to 15 minutes.

4.Units of Mass (Weight)

UnitSymbolEqual To
Milligrammg0.001 g
Gramg1,000 mg
Kilogramkg1,000 g
Tonnet1,000 kg

In everyday talk we say “weight”, but in science mass is the amount of matter in an object, and it is measured in grams and kilograms. A bag of sugar is about 1 kg. A mango is roughly 300 g. A large truck load might be measured in tonnes. A tonne here means the metric tonne of 1,000 kg.

5.Units of Capacity (Volume of Liquid)

UnitSymbolEqual To
MillilitremL0.001 L
LitreL1,000 mL
KilolitrekL1,000 L

A teaspoon holds about 5 mL, a glass of water about 250 mL, and a large bottle of drink 1.5 L. Capacity also links to volume: 1 mL = 1 cm³, so 1 L = 1,000 cm³, and 1 m³ = 1,000 L. One litre of water has a mass of about 1 kg, which is why cooks and scientists often treat millilitres and grams of water as equal.

6.The Conversion Pattern: A Ladder of Units

Picture every metric unit sitting on rungs of a ladder, biggest at the top. Moving down the ladder (bigger unit to smaller unit) means multiplying. Moving up the ladder (smaller unit to bigger unit) means dividing. The number you multiply or divide by is always the conversion factor between those two rungs.

The four rungs light up one after another. Red arrows go down (multiply), blue arrows go up (divide).
Quick Way to Remember

Smaller unit values mean you need more of them to describe the same amount (many small mm), while bigger unit values mean you need fewer of them (few large km). More of a smaller unit, fewer of a bigger unit: multiply going smaller, divide going bigger.

7.Converting Smaller Units to Bigger Units (Divide)

Example 1: Length

Convert 3,500 m to km. Since 1 km = 1,000 m, divide by 1,000: 3,500 ÷ 1,000 = 3.5 km.

Example 2: Mass

Convert 4,200 g to kg. Divide by 1,000: 4,200 ÷ 1,000 = 4.2 kg. And 350 mg to g: divide by 1,000 to get 0.35 g.

Example 3: Capacity

Convert 3,600 mL to L. Divide by 1,000: 3,600 ÷ 1,000 = 3.6 L.

8.Converting Bigger Units to Smaller Units (Multiply)

Example 4: Length

Convert 2.5 km to m. Multiply by 1,000: 2.5 × 1,000 = 2,500 m.

Example 5: Mass

Convert 0.8 tonne to kg. Multiply by 1,000: 0.8 × 1,000 = 800 kg.

Example 6: Capacity

Convert 2.75 L to mL. Multiply by 1,000: 2.75 × 1,000 = 2,750 mL.

Multiplying instead of dividing when converting mm to m

It is easy to forget which direction the operation goes once several units are involved.

The fix: going from a smaller unit to a bigger one always means dividing, because it takes many small units to make one big one. 4,500 mm to m: divide by 1,000 (since 1 m = 1,000 mm), giving 4.5 m, not 4,500,000 m.

A sense check that always works

Before you finish, ask: “Should my number get bigger or smaller?” If you change to a smaller unit (km to m), you need more of them, so the number must grow. If you change to a bigger unit (g to kg), the number must shrink. If your answer goes the wrong way, you used the wrong operation.

Example 7: Mixed Units

(a) Write 3 m 45 cm in centimetres: 3 m = 300 cm, so 300 + 45 = 345 cm.

(b) Write 2 kg 350 g in kilograms: 2 kg 350 g = 2,350 g ÷ 1,000 = 2.35 kg.

(c) Work out 5 m − 2 m 35 cm. Change everything to centimetres: 500 − 235 = 265 cm = 2 m 65 cm, or 2.65 m.

Never add or subtract numbers with different units. “2 m + 35 cm” is not 37 of anything.

9.Units of Time

Time is the one measurement that does not follow the metric system’s powers of 10. Its conversion factors come from history and astronomy instead (the 60 comes from an ancient counting system).

UnitEqual To
1 minute60 seconds
1 hour60 minutes = 3,600 seconds
1 day24 hours
1 week7 days
Example 8: Time Conversions

150 seconds = 2 minutes and 30 seconds (150 ÷ 60 = 2 remainder 30). 2.5 hours = 2.5 × 60 = 150 minutes. 3 days = 3 × 24 = 72 hours.

1.2 hours = 1 hour 20 minutes

Because the decimal point looks like “20”, many students read 0.2 hours as 20 minutes.

The fix: time is not in tens. Convert the decimal part by multiplying by 60. 0.2 hours = 0.2 × 60 = 12 minutes, so 1.2 hours = 1 hour 12 minutes. In the other direction, 3 h 45 min = 3 + 45/60 = 3.75 hours.

For clocks, the 12-hour and 24-hour systems and timetables, see the time page linked at the end of this article.

10.Units of Area: The Squaring Trap

Area units look similar to length units, but converting between them is not the same simple ×10 or ÷10 pattern. Area is length squared, so the conversion factor gets squared too.

Example 9

Since 1 m = 100 cm, squaring both sides gives 1 m² = 100 cm × 100 cm = 10,000 cm², not 100 cm² as the length conversion might suggest.

The gold square pulses to show one of the 100 small squares. The side is 100 times longer, but the area is 10,000 times bigger.
Converting 50,000 cm² to m² by dividing by 100

Applying the length conversion factor directly to an area is one of the most common measurement mistakes.

The fix: since 1 m² = 10,000 cm², divide by 10,000, not 100. 50,000 cm² ÷ 10,000 = 5 m². Any time a unit is squared (like cm² or m²), its conversion factor must be squared too.

UnitEqual To
1 m²10,000 cm²
1 hectare10,000 m² (a 100 m × 100 m square)
1 km²1,000,000 m²

The same idea works for volume, but with the factor cubed: 1 m³ = 100 × 100 × 100 = 1,000,000 cm³. Combined with 1 L = 1,000 cm³, this means 1 m³ = 1,000 L.

Example 10: Area and Volume

(a) Convert 3.5 m² to cm²: multiply by 10,000, giving 35,000 cm².

(b) A farm covers 2.5 hectares. In square metres that is 2.5 × 10,000 = 25,000 m².

(c) A water tank is 2 m long, 1.5 m wide and 0.8 m high. Volume = 2 × 1.5 × 0.8 = 2.4 m³. Capacity = 2.4 × 1,000 = 2,400 L.

11.Speed: A Unit Made of Two Units

Speed combines length and time, so its units do too: km/h means kilometres per hour, and m/s means metres per second. To change km/h to m/s, convert both parts: 1 km = 1,000 m and 1 h = 3,600 s, so 1 km/h = 1,000 ÷ 3,600 m/s. In practice this means divide by 3.6 to go from km/h to m/s, and multiply by 3.6 to go back.

Example 11

(a) 72 km/h = 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s.

(b) 15 m/s = 15 × 3.6 = 54 km/h.

12.Measurement in Real Life

Real-World Example

A marathon is 42,195 m long, usually written as 42.195 km. A recipe calling for 750 mL of milk is exactly 0.75 L, or three-quarters of a standard litre carton. A farm covering 2.5 hectares spans 2.5 × 10,000 = 25,000 m².

Example 12: Running Laps

A running track is 400 m around. How many laps make a 5 km run?

Step 1: 5 km = 5 × 1,000 = 5,000 m.

Step 2: 5,000 ÷ 400 = 12.5 laps.

Answer: 12.5 laps

Example 13: Packing Flour

A shop has 1.5 kg of flour and packs it in 250 g bags. How many bags can it fill?

1.5 kg = 1,500 g, and 1,500 ÷ 250 = 6 bags. Check: 6 × 250 g = 1,500 g. ✓

Example 14: Painting a Wall

A wall is 6 m long and 2.5 m high. One litre of paint covers 10 m² for one coat. How much paint is needed for two coats?

Area: 6 × 2.5 = 15 m². Two coats: 15 × 2 = 30 m². Paint: 30 ÷ 10 = 3 litres.

Example 15: How Long Will the Water Last?

The tank from Example 10 holds 2,400 L. A household uses 150 L a day. How many days will a full tank last?

2,400 ÷ 150 = 16 days.

Did You Know?

The metre was first proposed in France in the 1790s as one ten-millionth of the distance from the North Pole to the Equator, measured along a meridian through Paris. That was an ambitious attempt to base measurement on the Earth itself, rather than an arbitrary object. Today the metre is defined using the speed of light: it is the distance light travels in a vacuum in 1/299,792,458 of a second.

13.Common Mistakes

  • Going the wrong way. Smaller unit to bigger unit means divide. Bigger unit to smaller unit means multiply. Use the sense check: should the number grow or shrink?
  • Forgetting to square the factor for area. 1 m² is 10,000 cm², not 100 cm². For volume, cube the factor.
  • Treating time as decimal. 1.2 hours is 1 hour 12 minutes, not 1 hour 20 minutes.
  • Mixing units in one calculation. Change everything to the same unit before adding, subtracting, multiplying or dividing.
  • Dropping the unit. “12” could be 12 cm or 12 km. Always write the unit with the answer.
  • Mixing up m and mm, or L and mL. Read the symbol carefully. Capital L is litre and small m is metre or milli.
  • Converting km/h to m/s the wrong way. Divide by 3.6 to get m/s. If your m/s answer is bigger than the km/h number, check again.

15.Exam-Style Questions

  1. [2 marks] Convert 3.6 km to metres.
  2. [3 marks] A bottle holds 1.5 L of juice. How many 250 mL glasses can be filled from the bottle?
  3. [3 marks] A rectangular field is 250 m long and 160 m wide. Find its area in hectares.
  4. [4 marks] A car travels at a steady 90 km/h. Find its speed in m/s, and the distance it covers in 10 seconds.
  5. [4 marks] A tank is 1.2 m long, 0.5 m wide and 0.4 m high. Find its capacity in litres.
Show answers

1. 3.6 × 1,000 = 3,600 m.

2. 1.5 L = 1,500 mL. 1,500 ÷ 250 = 6 glasses.

3. Area = 250 × 160 = 40,000 m². Since 1 hectare = 10,000 m², the field is 40,000 ÷ 10,000 = 4 hectares.

4. 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s. Distance in 10 s = 25 × 10 = 250 m.

5. Volume = 1.2 × 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.24 m³. Capacity = 0.24 × 1,000 = 240 L.

16.Key Points

  • Every measurement needs a number and a unit.
  • Metric units use powers of 10: kilo = 1,000, centi = 1/100, milli = 1/1,000.
  • Bigger unit to smaller unit: multiply. Smaller unit to bigger unit: divide.
  • For area, square the length factor. For volume, cube it.
  • Time is not metric: 60 seconds, 60 minutes, 24 hours.
  • Convert everything to one unit before calculating, and check that your answer is sensible.

17.For Teachers and Parents

Students learn conversions best with real objects. Let them measure the desk in centimetres, weigh a bag of rice, and fill a jug using a 250 mL cup. Ask them to estimate first, then measure, then compare. When they convert, always ask “should the number get bigger or smaller?” Common trouble spots are area conversions and decimal time, so give extra practice with them. Everyday jobs such as cooking, shopping for fabric or paint, and planning a journey provide natural practice.

18.Why This Topic Matters

Unit conversion shows up everywhere: reading a recipe, following a doctor’s dosage instructions, understanding a weather report, buying fabric or paint by area, or reading distances on a road trip. It is also the essential first step before working with perimeter, area and volume formulas in geometry. You cannot calculate a shape’s measurements correctly if the units feeding into the formula do not match.
