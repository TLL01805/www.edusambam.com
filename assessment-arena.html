What is Assessment?

Assessment is the purposeful process of gathering evidence about learning and using that evidence to understand progress. It can show what a learner knows, what a learner can do, how well a concept has been understood, and which areas need further practice.

Good assessment is not simply about giving marks. It is a way of turning learning into useful information. A learner can use that information to make a better next step.

Importance of Assessment

Assessment gives learning direction. It can help learners identify strengths, discover gaps, practise difficult skills, monitor improvement and build confidence. For educators, assessment provides evidence that can guide teaching and support learners more effectively.

  • Checks understanding: reveals whether a concept has really been understood.
  • Identifies learning gaps: shows where more explanation or practice is needed.
  • Strengthens memory: retrieval and practice can help learners recall and apply knowledge.
  • Builds confidence: visible progress can motivate learners to continue.
  • Supports improvement: results can guide the next round of learning and practice.

What is Self-Assessment?

Self-assessment is the process in which a learner examines their own knowledge, skills, performance or progress. Instead of waiting for someone else to say whether the learning is strong, the learner takes an active role in judging what they understand and what they need to improve.

A useful habit: After learning a concept, ask yourself: Can I explain it in my own words? Can I use it in a new situation? Can I answer questions without looking at the notes?

Kinds of Assessments

Assessment can take different forms because learning can be checked in different ways.

  • Diagnostic assessment: used before or at the beginning of learning to identify prior knowledge, strengths and gaps.
  • Formative assessment: used during learning to provide feedback and guide improvement.
  • Summative assessment: used after a period of learning to evaluate achievement against defined expectations.
  • Self-assessment: completed by learners to reflect on their own understanding and performance.
  • Peer assessment: learners review one another's work using clear criteria and constructive feedback.
  • Performance or practical assessment: evaluates the ability to apply knowledge or skills through a task, demonstration or real-world activity.

How to Use the Assessment Arena

  1. Learn the concept and methodology rather than memorising a syllabus.
  2. Practise with questions and activities.
  3. Enter the Assessment Arena when you feel ready to test your understanding.
  4. Complete the assessment carefully and review your result.
  5. Return to the learning resource for topics that need strengthening.
  6. Try again and use improvement as motivation for the next stage.

Assessment with Understanding

Assessment becomes most useful when it is connected to learning. A score alone does not explain everything. Understanding the mistakes, revisiting the concept, practising again and trying a new assessment can turn assessment into a powerful learning cycle.

Learn → Understand → Practise → Assess → Reflect → Improve.
