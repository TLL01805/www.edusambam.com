Synonyms in English: A Complete Guide with Examples

Happy, glad, delighted. Big, large, enormous. Learn how synonyms differ in strength, formality and feeling, and choose the exact word every time.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team | 22 min read | Last updated: November 2026 | Vocabulary

A rich vocabulary is not about knowing difficult words. It is about choosing the exact word for the moment. Synonyms, words with the same or nearly the same meaning, give you that choice, and they are one of the quickest ways to make your speech and writing clearer, more precise and more interesting.

This guide explains what synonyms are, why true synonyms are rare, and how to choose between them. Its main part gives 167 common words with 698 synonyms and an example sentence for each, grouped by theme. It also includes a table of weak words with stronger replacements, common mistakes, key points, ten practice questions and a teacher activity.

It builds on Parts of Speech and connects with Phrasal Verbs, Formal vs. Informal English and Common Errors in English. Spelling in this guide follows British English.

1.What Is a Synonym?

A synonym is a word that has the same, or almost the same, meaning as another word in the same language. The word comes from Greek roots meaning "with the name". For example, begin and start, rapid and swift, enormous and huge are synonyms.

Synonyms can be nouns, verbs, adjectives or adverbs, but a word and its synonym must belong to the same part of speech: happy (adjective) matches cheerful (adjective), not happiness (noun).

2.Why Synonyms Are Rarely Identical

Very few words mean exactly the same in every situation. Words that look alike usually differ in one of these ways, which is why a thesaurus must be used with care.

DifferenceExampleWhat it shows
Strengthpleased, happy, delighted, overjoyedThe words rise in strength of feeling.
Formalitybuy and purchase; start and commenceThe second word in each pair is more formal.
Attitude (connotation)thrifty and stingy; determined and stubbornEach pair has a similar meaning but praises or criticises.
Common word partnersa heavy rain, not a strong rain; a strong tea, not a heavy teaSome words are only used with certain partners.
Regionlift (British) and elevator (American); autumn and fallSome synonyms belong to a different region.
Grammar patternsay, tellHe told me but He said to me; the patterns differ.

3.Types of Synonyms

TypeMeaningExample
Absolute (true) synonymsIdentical in meaning in all contexts; very raresofa and couch (in most uses)
Near-synonymsVery close but different in strength or feelingangry, furious, irritated
Contextual synonymsSynonyms only in a particular contextfair and just (a fair or just decision), but not a fair complexion
Formal and informal pairsSame meaning, different stylekids and children; buy and purchase

4.How to Choose the Right Synonym

  1. Check the meaning. Be sure that the new word means what you want. Walk and stagger are both movement verbs, but they are not equal.
  2. Check the strength. Do you need upset, distressed or devastated?
  3. Check the register. A formal essay needs commence; a message to a friend needs start.
  4. Check the attitude. Slim is positive, skinny can be negative.
  5. Check the word partners. Say the phrase aloud. If it sounds wrong, look it up.
  6. Check an example. Read the dictionary example before you use a new word in your own sentence.

15.Common Mistakes with Synonyms

MistakeBetter habit
Using a long word you do not fully understandUse a synonym only after checking its meaning in a dictionary.
Ignoring formality (The kids commenced the activity)Match the word to the situation: The children began the activity.
Ignoring attitude (He is a stingy manager when you mean careful)Choose a word with the right attitude: thrifty or careful.
Wrong word partners (do a mistake)Use make a mistake; learn words with their partners.
Wrong grammar pattern (He explained me the rule)Use He explained the rule to me.
Changing every simple wordKeep simple words when they are clear; use synonyms to avoid repetition.
Key points to remember
  • A synonym has the same or nearly the same meaning as another word of the same part of speech.
  • True synonyms are rare; most differ in strength, formality, attitude, word partners or region.
  • Check meaning, strength, register, attitude and word partners before choosing a synonym.
  • Replace very + adjective with one stronger word in formal writing.
  • Use a dictionary example to confirm that a new word fits your sentence.
  • Keep simple words when they are clear.
For teachers and parents

Activity: give students a short paragraph full of plain words such as good, big, said and ask them to replace each with a more exact synonym, then compare choices in pairs and explain which word is stronger or more formal. A synonym ladder (for example tired, weary, exhausted) helps younger students understand strength.

Please note: this guide uses British spelling, and the synonyms shown are common choices, not complete lists. The best synonym always depends on the sentence.
