EQ Test: Online Emotional Intelligence

24 situations on messages, social media and online conflict. Choose the best response and learn why.

Much of life now happens through screens, where tone, faces and pauses are missing. This auto-graded test gives you 24 situations about messages, social media and online conflict. Tap the best response, see the reason immediately, and finish with a score and a next step.

Key Takeaways

Messages carry less emotional information than voices and faces, and research suggests that senders overestimate how clearly their tone comes across. People also sometimes say harsher things online than they would face to face. Pausing before you send, checking facts before you share, asking permission before you post about others, and shaping your feed are habits that protect both you and other people.

1.How This Test Works

Part 1 covers messages and tone, Part 2 covers social media and your feelings, and Part 3 covers online conflict, safety and kindness. The best answers favour a pause before sending, clear wording, privacy and respect, and telling a trusted adult when something threatening or bullying happens. The tool explains every answer and links to a related article. Nothing is saved, and the answers reshuffle each time.

3.Using Your Result

Choose one habit to try this week, such as waiting five minutes before sending an angry message, checking the source before sharing a claim, or muting one account that leaves you feeling worse. If someone is threatening you or targeting you online, keep screenshots, block and report them, and tell a trusted adult. You do not have to handle it alone.

Sources and Evidence

  • Suler, J. (2004). The online disinhibition effect. CyberPsychology and Behavior, 7(3), 321-326.
  • Kross, E. et al. (2013). Facebook use predicts declines in subjective well-being in young adults. PLoS ONE, 8(8), e69841.
