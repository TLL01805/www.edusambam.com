How to Identify Blood Groups

Everyone belongs to one of four main blood groups, A, B, AB or O, and is also Rh positive (+) or Rh negative (−). Together these make eight blood types. A group is decided by antigens, which are markers on the surface of red blood cells.

Antigen + matching antibody → red cells clump together (agglutination)

To find a blood group, a laboratory mixes drops of blood with three test liquids called anti-A, anti-B and anti-D. Clumping shows which antigens are present. This is a classroom model. Real blood typing is done by trained laboratory staff.

  1. Learn the antigens and antibodies of each group.
  2. Add blood to the three test wells.
  3. Read the reactions.
  4. Name the blood group.
  5. Choose safe blood donors for the patient.
