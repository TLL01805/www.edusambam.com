Root Words, Prefixes and Suffixes: Word Parts That Build Vocabulary

Un-, re-, -tion, spect, graph. Learn the Latin and Greek roots, prefixes and suffixes that unlock the meaning of thousands of English words.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team | 26 min read | Last updated: November 2026 | Vocabulary

Why does unbelievable mean "not able to be believed"? Why do telephone, television and telescope all begin with tele-? Most English words are built from small parts, and if you learn the parts, you can read and spell thousands of words, including words you have never seen before.

This guide explains the three kinds of word parts (roots, prefixes and suffixes) and shows you how to break words apart to find their meaning. Its main part gives 198 word parts with 591 example words: 52 prefixes, 39 suffixes, 72 Latin roots and 35 Greek roots. It also includes twenty worked examples, spelling changes, key points, ten practice questions and a teacher activity.

It connects with Synonyms in English, Antonyms in English, One-Word Substitution and Commonly Misspelled Words. Spelling in this guide follows British English.

1.Roots, Prefixes and Suffixes

Word partWhat it isExample
RootThe core part that carries the main meaningspect (look) in inspect, spectator
PrefixA part added at the beginning; it changes the meaningun- in unhappy, re- in rewrite
SuffixA part added at the end; it often changes the part of speech-ness in kindness, -ly in slowly
Base wordA whole word that can stand alonehappy, believe, form

A word may have all three parts. For example, unbelievable = un- (not) + believe (accept as true) + -able (able to be). Many English roots come from Latin and Greek, which is why academic and scientific words contain them. Everyday words come mostly from Old English.

2.How to Work Out an Unknown Word

  1. Cover the prefix and the suffix. Find the root in the middle.
  2. Think of the root's meaning. Use the tables below.
  3. Add the prefix meaning. What does it add or change?
  4. Add the suffix meaning. Is the word a noun, verb, adjective or adverb?
  5. Check with the sentence. Does the meaning fit the context?
  6. Check a dictionary because some words have changed meaning over time.

8.Spelling Changes When Adding Word Parts

RuleExamples
Drop a silent e before a vowel suffixcreate + -ive → creative, hope + -ing → hoping
Keep the e before a consonant suffixcare + -ful → careful, move + -ment → movement
Change y to i before a suffixhappy + -ness → happiness, beauty + -ful → beautiful
Double the last letter after one stressed vowelbegin + -ing → beginning, admit + -ed → admitted
Prefix ending in a letter that matches the rootil- + legal → illegal, ir- + regular → irregular, mis- + spell → misspell
Assimilation: the prefix changes to fit the rootin- + possible → impossible, com- + mit → commit, ad- + tract → attract

9.Common Mistakes with Word Parts

MistakeCorrectWhy
inpossibleimpossibleThe prefix in- becomes im- before p.
unregularirregularUse ir- before r.
disatisfieddissatisfiedDis- + satisfied keeps both s's.
misspell as mispellmisspellMis- + spell keeps both s's.
carefullcarefulThe suffix -ful has one l.
useingusingDrop the silent e before -ing.
happynesshappinessChange y to i before -ness.
beginning as beginingbeginningDouble the n before -ing.
assuming that every in- word means not (invaluable, inflammable)check the dictionarySome in- words mean "very" or "into".
Key points to remember
  • A word can have a prefix, a root and a suffix.
  • Prefixes change meaning; suffixes often change the part of speech.
  • Many roots come from Latin and Greek: dict (say), spect (look), port (carry), graph (write), bio (life).
  • Learn the common prefixes and suffixes first, then the roots.
  • Use the parts to guess unknown words, then check a dictionary.
  • Watch spelling changes when you join parts: impossible, dissatisfied, happiness.
For teachers and parents

Activity: give each student a list of ten long words and ask them to split each into prefix, root and suffix, and then to guess the meaning before checking. A word-building game works well: give cards with prefixes, roots and suffixes, and ask teams to build as many real words as they can in five minutes.

Please note: this guide uses British spelling. Word meanings can change over time, so a word may differ slightly from its parts. Students should always confirm the meaning in a dictionary.
