Commonly Mispronounced Words in English: A Guide with Respellings and Audio

Colonel, yacht, Wednesday, February. Learn the correct pronunciation of the words that cause the most mistakes, with respellings, stress patterns and audio.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team | 24 min read | Last updated: November 2026 | Vocabulary

Many of the most common words in English are not pronounced the way they are spelled. Colonel sounds like kernel. Yacht rhymes with hot. Wednesday has three syllables, not four. If you pronounce a word wrongly, listeners may not understand you, even if your spelling and grammar are perfect.

This guide explains why English pronunciation is so irregular, how to read the simple respellings used in the tables, and the most common patterns of mistakes. Its main part gives 111 commonly mispronounced words in five groups, each with a respelling and a note, plus a speaker button to hear the word. It also includes 22 words that change pronunciation with meaning, 14 words with British and American differences, 12 British place names, the many sounds of -ough, rules for -ed and -s endings, key points, ten practice questions and a teacher activity.

It connects with Commonly Misspelled Words, Commonly Confused Words and Common Errors in English. The pronunciations given are standard British English.

1.Why Is English Pronunciation Difficult?

  • Spelling does not match sound. The same letters can sound different (cough, through, though, tough) and the same sound can be spelled in many ways.
  • Silent letters. Many words keep letters that were once pronounced: knife, doubt, psychology.
  • Stress changes meaning. Record as a noun and record as a verb are pronounced differently.
  • Borrowed words. Words from French, Latin, Greek and other languages keep their original sounds: ballet, genre, niche.
  • Different accents. There is no single "correct" accent. British, American, Australian, Indian and Pakistani speakers all pronounce some words differently. What matters is being clear and understood.

12.How to Improve Your Pronunciation

  • Listen first. Hear the word from a good dictionary or a news broadcast before you say it.
  • Break the word into syllables and mark the stress: pho-TOG-ra-phy.
  • Record yourself and compare with a model.
  • Do not trust spelling. Check the pronunciation of every new word in a dictionary.
  • Practise in sentences, not only single words.
  • Learn word families. If you know photograph, notice how the stress moves in photography.
  • Read aloud daily for five minutes.
Key points to remember
  • English spelling and pronunciation do not always match; check new words in a dictionary.
  • Many mistakes come from silent letters, wrong stress and extra or missing syllables.
  • Stress can change the meaning and the part of speech: REK-ord (noun), ri-KORD (verb).
  • -ed endings are pronounced /t/, /d/ or /id/; -s endings are /s/, /z/ or /iz/.
  • The spelling -ough has several sounds: through, though, thought, tough, cough, bough.
  • British and American pronunciations can differ; both are correct in their own region.
  • Clear speech matters more than a particular accent.
For teachers and parents

Activity: give each student a list of ten words from the tables and ask them to read each aloud, then to listen to the speaker button and compare. Follow with a minimal-pair game, such as live / leave or ship / sheep, in which the teacher says one word and students choose the one they heard. Record the class reading a short passage and play it back for self-correction.

Please note: pronunciations in this guide are standard British English, shown in a simple respelling that is not the same as the International Phonetic Alphabet. Accents vary widely, and a dictionary with audio recordings is the best final check.
