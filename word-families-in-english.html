Word Families in English: Noun, Verb, Adjective and Adverb Forms

Decide, decision, decisive, decisively. Learn how one word becomes four, with noun, verb, adjective and adverb forms, suffix rules and practice for essays and exams.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team | 24 min read | Last updated: November 2026 | Vocabulary

If you know the word decide, you already know decision, decisive and decisively. This group is a word family: a base word and the other words formed from it, each with a different part of speech. Learning words in families is one of the fastest ways to build your vocabulary, because one new word gives you four or five.

This guide explains what word families are, how suffixes show the part of speech, and the spelling changes that happen when words change form. Its main part gives 178 word families in five themed tables, each in four columns: noun, verb, adjective and adverb. It also covers people nouns, adverbs with different meanings, words formed from the same base with different meanings, fifteen practice sentences with answers, ways to use word families to improve essays, common mistakes, key points, ten practice questions and a teacher activity.

It builds on Parts of Speech and connects with Root Words, Prefixes and Suffixes, Degrees of Adjectives, Collocations in English and Commonly Misspelled Words. Spelling in this guide follows British English.

1.What Is a Word Family?

A word family is a group of words that share the same base and are related in meaning, but have different forms and different jobs in a sentence.

Part of speechFormJob in a sentenceExample sentence
Noundecisionnames a thing or ideaIt was a wise decision.
Verbdecideshows an actionWe decide tomorrow.
Adjectivedecisivedescribes a nounShe is a decisive leader.
Adverbdecisivelydescribes a verbShe acted decisively.

Not every family has all four forms. Some have no verb (happiness, happy, happily) and some have no adverb (pollution, pollute, polluted). In the tables, a dash (—) means that no common form exists.

2.Why Word Families Matter

  • Essays. You can change the form of a word to fit the sentence, which makes your writing more varied and more formal.
  • Word formation tasks. Many English examinations ask you to change the form of a word given in brackets to fit a gap. This task tests word families directly.
  • Reading. Seeing a familiar base inside a long word helps you guess its meaning.
  • Accuracy. You avoid mistakes such as He is a success man, where the adjective successful is needed.

3.How Suffixes Show the Part of Speech

The ending of a word is often the best clue to its part of speech.

Part of speechCommon endingsExamples
Noun-tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ance, -ence, -ism, -ship, -hooddecision, movement, kindness, ability, importance
Verb-ise (-ize), -ify, -en, -aterealise, simplify, strengthen, activate
Adjective-ful, -less, -ous, -al, -ive, -ic, -able, -ible, -y, -ent, -anthelpful, famous, national, active, rainy
Adverb-ly, -ally, -ily, -ably, -iblyquickly, basically, happily, reliably

4.Spelling Changes in Word Families

ChangeExamples
Drop silent e before a vowel endingcreate → creation, decide → decision, admire → admirable
Change y to i before an endinghappy → happiness, happily; beauty → beautiful
Add -ally to words ending in -icbasic → basically, economic → economically (exception: public → publicly)
-le becomes -lypossible → possibly, simple → simply
-ue becomes -ulytrue → truly, due → duly
Verb ends in -ifysimple → simplify, pure → purify, clear → clarify
The stress can movePHOtograph, phoTOGraphy, photoGRAPHic
The root may changedecide → decision, explain → explanation, compete → competition

12.Words from the Same Base with Different Meanings

Several adjectives come from the same base but mean different things. Learn each separately.

WordsDifference
economic / economicalEconomic relates to the economy; economical means saving money.
historic / historicalHistoric means important in history; historical relates to history.
respectful / respectable / respectiveRespectful shows respect; respectable deserves respect; respective means belonging to each one separately.
sensible / sensitiveSensible shows good sense; sensitive is easily affected.
imaginary / imaginativeImaginary means not real; imaginative means full of ideas.
successful / successiveSuccessful means achieving the aim; successive means one after another.
considerable / considerateConsiderable means large; considerate means thoughtful of others.
comprehensive / comprehensibleComprehensive means complete; comprehensible means able to be understood.
electric / electricalElectric is used with specific things (an electric guitar); electrical is used in general (electrical engineering).
bored / boring, interested / interesting-ed describes how a person feels; -ing describes what causes the feeling.

14.Using Word Families to Improve Your Essays

Changing a verb or adjective into a noun is a common feature of formal writing. It makes sentences shorter and more academic.

Simple sentenceMore formal version
We decided quickly.Our quick decision saved time.
Many people are polluting rivers.The pollution of rivers is increasing.
Teachers educate students carefully.The careful education of students matters.
The government acted fast.The government's fast action surprised everyone.
It is important to protect forests.The protection of forests is important.
Prices rose sharply.There was a sharp rise in prices.
Students who revise regularly succeed.Regular revision leads to success.
  • Vary the form. Do not repeat important three times; use importance and importantly where they fit.
  • Use the noun after "the". The protection of children sounds more formal than protecting children.
  • Check the adverb. An adverb describes a verb, adjective or another adverb, not a noun.

15.Common Mistakes with Word Forms

MistakeCorrectWhy
She is a success woman.She is a successful woman.An adjective is needed before the noun.
He sings beautiful.He sings beautifully.An adverb describes the verb.
I am very interest in history.I am very interested in history.Use the adjective after am.
The film was bored.The film was boring.-ing describes the cause; -ed describes the feeling.
The decide was hard.The decision was hard.A noun is needed after the.
She spoke confident.She spoke confidently.An adverb describes the verb.
Pollution is danger.Pollution is dangerous.An adjective is needed after is.
He is a really difference person.He is a really different person.Use the adjective different.
It is importance to study.It is important to study.An adjective is needed after is.
She hardly worked all night.She worked hard all night.Hardly means almost not.
Key points to remember
  • A word family is a base word with its noun, verb, adjective and adverb forms.
  • Suffixes show the part of speech: -tion and -ness (nouns), -ise and -ify (verbs), -ful and -ous (adjectives), -ly (adverbs).
  • Learn each new word with its whole family.
  • Watch the spelling changes: decide → decision, happy → happiness, true → truly.
  • Some adverbs change meaning with -ly: hard / hardly, late / lately, near / nearly.
  • Choose the form by the job in the sentence: noun after the, adjective before a noun, adverb with a verb.
  • Use word families to vary your essay style, but check the form every time.
For teachers and parents

Activity: give each student ten base words and a blank table with four columns (noun, verb, adjective, adverb), and ask them to fill in the forms using a dictionary. Next, ask them to write one sentence for each form. For older students, give a short paragraph with gaps and bracketed words and ask them to complete it, as in an examination word formation task.

Please note: this guide uses British spelling. Some families have rare or informal forms that are not shown, and a dash means that no common form exists. Students should check a dictionary for any form they are unsure of.
