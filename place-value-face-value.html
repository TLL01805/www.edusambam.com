Place Value & Face Value: International, Indian & Pakistani Systems

The same digit can be worth 5 or 5 crore, depending only on where it sits. Learn how position gives value, and how lakh, crore and arab fit with million and billion.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team|11 min read|Last updated: October 2026|Mathematics

Every digit in a number tells two stories: what it looks like on its own, and what it is worth because of where it sits. The same digit 5 can mean 5 rupees or 5 crore rupees. Understanding both stories, and how they change between the International system and the Indian and Pakistani system, is the foundation for reading any large number correctly.

1.What Is Face Value?

The face value of a digit is simply the digit itself. It does not depend on where the digit stands in the number. The face value of 7 is always 7, whether it sits in the ones place or the millions place.

Example 1

In the number 7,392, the face value of 7 is 7 and the face value of 3 is 3. Position never changes face value.

2.What Is Place Value?

The place value of a digit is what it is worth because of its position. It equals the digit multiplied by the value of its column (ones, tens, hundreds and so on).

Example 2

In 7,392, the digit 7 is in the thousands column, so its place value is 7 × 1,000 = 7,000. The digit 3 is in the hundreds column, so its place value is 3 × 100 = 300. The digit 9 is in the tens column: 9 × 10 = 90. The digit 2 is in the ones column: 2 × 1 = 2.

Check: 7,000 + 300 + 90 + 2 = 7,392. ✓

“The place value of 7 in 7,392 is just 7”

This mixes up face value (the digit alone) with place value (the digit’s worth based on position). It is the most common confusion in this topic.

The fix: the face value of 7 is 7. The place value of 7 in 7,392 is 7,000, because it sits in the thousands column. Same digit, two different questions, two different answers.

Expanded form

Writing a number as the sum of the place values of its digits is called expanded form. Zeros are left out, because their place value is 0.

Example 3

Write 3,406,052 in expanded form.

3,406,052 = 3,000,000 + 400,000 + 6,000 + 50 + 2.

The zero in the ten-thousands column and the zero in the hundreds column have a place value of 0, so we do not write them. Check: 3,000,000 + 400,000 = 3,400,000; + 6,000 = 3,406,000; + 50 = 3,406,050; + 2 = 3,406,052. ✓

3.The International Place Value System

The International system is used in the USA, the UK and most of the world. It groups digits in sets of three, separated by commas: ones, thousands, millions, billions.

Comma rule: starting from the right, place a comma after every group of 3 digits. The number 342567891 becomes 342,567,891 (three hundred and forty-two million, five hundred and sixty-seven thousand, eight hundred and ninety-one).

4.The Indian and Pakistani Place Value System

India, Pakistan and nearby countries also use a second grouping system. It is built around the lakh (100,000) and the crore (10,000,000). It is common in everyday use, newspapers and money amounts in these countries.

Comma rule: the rightmost group has 3 digits, and every group after that has only 2 digits. The number 342567891 becomes 34,25,67,891 (thirty-four crore, twenty-five lakh, sixty-seven thousand, eight hundred and ninety-one).

The names continue upwards: 100 arab is 1 kharab (1011). The next names are used rarely, so for school work you mainly need lakh, crore and arab.

International grouping (red brackets) uses threes. Indian grouping (gold brackets) uses 3, then twos.
Writing 34,567,891 in the Indian system by copying the International commas

Comma placement is not interchangeable. Reading Indian place names with International commas, or the other way round, gives the wrong number.

The fix: regroup the digits using the system’s own rule. The digits 34567891 are 34,567,891 in the International system (34 million). In the Indian system the same digits are 3,45,67,891 (3 crore 45 lakh). Never mix the two.

Quick Conversions

100 thousand = 1 lakh. 100 lakh = 1 crore. 100 crore = 1 arab. 100 arab = 1 kharab. Every “hundred” of one unit makes the next unit, which is a very regular pattern.

Example 4: International to Indian

Rewrite 53,482,917 using Indian commas and say it in words.

Step 1: Write the digits without commas: 53482917.

Step 2: From the right, take 3 digits (917), then groups of 2: 82, 34, 5.

Answer: 5,34,82,917, which is 5 crore 34 lakh 82 thousand 917.

Example 5: Indian to International

Rewrite 7,25,60,000 using International commas.

Step 1: Digits: 72560000. This is 7 crore 25 lakh 60 thousand.

Step 2: Group in threes from the right: 000, 560, 72.

Answer: 72,560,000, which is 72 million 560 thousand. Check: 7 crore = 70 million, and 25 lakh = 2.5 million, so 72.5 million plus 60 thousand = 72.56 million. ✓

Real-World Example: Reading Money Amounts

A project is reported as costing “45 arab rupees”. In the Indian and Pakistani system, 45 arab is written 45,00,00,00,000. In the International system the same amount is 45,000,000,000, which is 45 billion rupees. The amount is the same. Only the grouping and names are different. Notice that the figure is not 4.5 billion: 1 arab is 1 billion, so 45 arab is 45 billion. This is exactly the kind of slip that place value helps you avoid.

Example 6: A Salary in Lakhs

A person earns Rs 85,000 a month. How much is that in a year, written with Indian commas?

Step 1: 85,000 × 12 = 1,020,000.

Step 2: Regroup: 10,20,000.

Answer: Rs 10,20,000, which is 10 lakh 20 thousand.

6.Comparing and Rounding Large Numbers

To compare two numbers, compare the digits from the left, one place at a time. The first place where they differ decides which number is bigger.

Example 7: Which Is Bigger?

Compare 5,09,87,654 and 5,10,00,000.

Both have 5 crore. Next, compare the lakhs: the first number has 09 lakh and the second has 10 lakh. Since 10 is more than 09, the second number is bigger.

Check in International form: 50,987,654 and 51,000,000. ✓

Answer: 5,10,00,000 is bigger.

Example 8: Rounding to a Place

Round 6,48,35,912 (that is 64,835,912) to the nearest lakh and to the nearest crore.

Nearest lakh: look at the digits after the lakh place: 35,912. This is less than 50,000, so round down: 6,48,00,000.

Nearest crore: the number is 6.48 crore. Since 0.48 is less than 0.5, round down: 6,00,00,000.

7.Place Value After the Decimal Point

Place value continues to the right of the decimal point. Each place is ten times smaller than the one before it: tenths, hundredths, thousandths.

Example 9

In 4.708, the digit 4 is in the ones place (value 4). The digit 7 is in the tenths place (value 0.7). The digit 0 is in the hundredths place (value 0). The digit 8 is in the thousandths place (value 0.008).

Check: 4 + 0.7 + 0 + 0.008 = 4.708. ✓

The steps rise one after another. A bigger place means a bigger value for the same digit.

8.Common Mistakes

  • Mixing up face value and place value. Face value is the digit; place value is the digit times its column value.
  • Using International commas with Indian names. Always regroup the digits for the system you are using.
  • Thinking zero has no meaning. The place value of 0 is 0, but the zero holds the other digits in their correct columns. Without it, 4,072 would become 472.
  • Miscounting places in long numbers. Count from the right, not the left.
  • Confusing 1 arab and 1 million. 1 arab = 1,00,00,00,000 = 1 billion, which is 1,000 times bigger than a million.
  • Forgetting repeated digits. In 5,158 the digit 5 appears twice. The thousands 5 is worth 5,000 and the tens 5 is worth 50. Always name the position.

1. The face value of 6 is 6. In 4,602,315 the 6 is in the hundred-thousands place, so its place value is 600,000.

2. Digits 24683057: groups from the right are 057, 83, 46, 2, so it is 2,46,83,057.

3. 1 crore = 100 lakh, so 2.5 crore = 2.5 × 100 = 250 lakh.

10.Exam-Style Questions

  1. [2 marks] Write the place value of the digit 8 in 3,082,945.
  2. [3 marks] Write 5,00,03,407 in words in the Indian system and write it with International commas.
  3. [3 marks] Write 2,350,000 as a number of lakhs.
  4. [4 marks] A company earns Rs 12,50,00,000 in a year. Write this amount in the International system and say how many millions it is. Then say how many crore it is.
Show answers

1. 3,082,945: the 8 is in the ten-thousands place, so its place value is 80,000.

2. 5,00,03,407 is 5 crore, 0 lakh, 3 thousand, 4 hundred and 7: five crore three thousand four hundred and seven. In International commas: 50,003,407.

3. 2,350,000 ÷ 100,000 = 23.5, so it is 23.5 lakh.

4. 12,50,00,000 has digits 125000000, so International form is 125,000,000. That is 125 million. In crore: 125,000,000 ÷ 10,000,000 = 12.5 crore.

11.Key Points

  • Face value is the digit itself. Place value is the digit multiplied by the value of its column.
  • The International system groups in threes (thousand, million, billion). The Indian and Pakistani system groups 3, then 2s (thousand, lakh, crore, arab).
  • 1 lakh = 100 thousand, 1 crore = 10 million, 1 arab = 1 billion.
  • When converting, remove the commas, then regroup for the other system.
  • To compare numbers, check digits from the left, one place at a time.
  • Place value continues after the decimal point: tenths, hundredths, thousandths.

12.For Teachers and Parents

A physical place-value chart, or even a chart drawn on paper with columns, helps students see why the digit 0 is needed. Ask learners to read money amounts they meet in daily life (fees, prices of a phone or a motorbike, newspaper figures) in both systems. Students who live in South Asia often hear lakh and crore at home but write International commas at school, so make the two systems explicit and practise converting. Encourage students to say the number aloud in words, which exposes mistakes such as reading 1,00,000 as “ten thousand”.

13.Did You Know?

Did You Know?

The words “lakh” and “crore” come from older Indian words (laksha and koti) and have been used in South Asia for a very long time. The grouping of commas, however, is a separate matter of printing style, and different regions use different habits.
