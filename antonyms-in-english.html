Antonyms in English: A Complete Guide with Examples

Hot and cold, rise and fall, buy and sell. Learn the four types of antonyms, how prefixes form opposites, and the traps that catch even good writers.

EDUSAMBAM Editorial Team | 22 min read | Last updated: November 2026 | Vocabulary

Hot and cold, rise and fall, buy and sell. Antonyms, words with opposite meanings, are all around us, and they help us to describe, compare and argue clearly. Learning antonyms doubles your vocabulary quickly, because every new word you learn can be paired with its opposite.

This guide explains what antonyms are, the four main types, how prefixes and suffixes form antonyms, and how to avoid common traps. Its main part gives 161 antonym pairs with example sentences, grouped by theme, plus a table of prefix antonyms. It also includes words that only look like antonyms, mistakes, key points, ten practice questions and a teacher activity.

It pairs naturally with Synonyms in English and builds on Parts of Speech, Degrees of Adjectives and Common Errors in English. Spelling in this guide follows British English.

1.What Is an Antonym?

An antonym is a word that has the opposite meaning to another word. The term comes from Greek roots meaning "against" and "name". Big and small, ancient and modern, and arrive and depart are antonyms. A word and its antonym must belong to the same part of speech.

Many words have no true antonym (for example table or river), and some have more than one, depending on the meaning.

2.The Four Types of Antonyms

TypeHow it worksExamples
GradableOpposite ends of a scale, with possible middle points; the words can be modified by very or comparedhot / cold (warm and cool lie between); happy / sad; tall / short
ComplementaryTwo possibilities only, with no middle; one excludes the otheralive / dead; true / false; on / off; pass / fail
Relational (converse)Each word describes the same relationship from the opposite sidebuy / sell; teacher / student; parent / child; above / below
Reversive (directional)Movement or action in opposite directions, or an action and its undoingenter / exit; up / down; tie / untie; rise / fall

A quick test: if you can say not hot, but not cold either, the pair is gradable. If not alive must mean dead, the pair is complementary.

3.Words with More Than One Antonym

When a word has several meanings, each meaning can have its own antonym. Always choose the antonym that fits the sentence.

4.Forming Antonyms with Prefixes and Suffixes

Many antonyms are made by adding a prefix to the word. The prefix you need depends on the word, and there is no single rule, so check a dictionary when unsure. These patterns cover most cases.

Prefix or suffixMeaningExamples
un-not; the reverse ofhappy → unhappy, fair → unfair, kind → unkind, known → unknown, lock → unlock, tie → untie
in-notactive → inactive, complete → incomplete, correct → incorrect, direct → indirect, visible → invisible
im- (before m, p, b)notpossible → impossible, polite → impolite, patient → impatient, mature → immature, balance → imbalance
il- (before l)notlegal → illegal, logical → illogical, literate → illiterate, legible → illegible
ir- (before r)notregular → irregular, relevant → irrelevant, responsible → irresponsible, replaceable → irreplaceable
dis-not; the opposite ofagree → disagree, honest → dishonest, appear → disappear, obey → disobey, connect → disconnect
non-notsense → nonsense, fiction → non-fiction, stop → non-stop, smoker → non-smoker
a-without; notmoral → amoral, typical → atypical, symmetrical → asymmetrical
de-to undo or reverseactivate → deactivate, code → decode, frost → defrost, value → devalue
anti-againstclockwise → anticlockwise, social → antisocial, climax → anticlimax
-ful / -lesswith / withoutcareful → careless, hopeful → hopeless, useful → useless, helpful → helpless, painful → painless

Useful rules

  • Before m, p and b, the prefix in- becomes im- (impossible, immature, imbalance).
  • Before l and r, it becomes il- and ir- (illegal, irregular).
  • Do not change the spelling of the base word. Natural becomes unnatural, with a double n, because un- ends in n and natural begins with n.
  • Use a hyphen with non- before many words (non-fiction, non-stop) and when the base word begins with a capital letter.
  • Do not invent prefix antonyms. There is no unbig or inhappy. Use the real antonym (small, sad).

13.Using Antonyms in Writing

  • Contrast. Antonyms make a point clear: Some students prefer to work alone; others thrive in groups.
  • Balance. Pairing opposites creates a memorable pattern: Easy to say, hard to do.
  • Precision. Say not unusual instead of common only when you want a softer, more careful tone.
  • Figures of speech. Oxymoron joins opposites on purpose: deafening silence.
Key points to remember
  • An antonym is a word with the opposite meaning, of the same part of speech.
  • The four types are gradable, complementary, relational and reversive.
  • Many antonyms are formed with un-, in-, im-, il-, ir-, dis-, non-, a-, de-, anti- and -less.
  • Use im- before m, p, b; il- before l; ir- before r.
  • A word with several meanings can have several antonyms, so check the meaning.
  • Watch for traps: invaluable and inflammable do not mean "not".
For teachers and parents

Activity: give students ten adjectives and ask them to write the antonym, then to say whether the pair is gradable or complementary. Next, ask them to write a sentence using both words, such as The road was wide at the start but narrow near the village. Finish with a game of "opposite day", in which the class answers each prompt with its antonym.

Please note: this guide uses British spelling, and the antonyms shown are the most common choices. The best antonym depends on the meaning in the sentence.
