What is Artificial Intelligence and How Does It Work?

Artificial Intelligence appears in news headlines daily and is already built into tools billions of people use every day — often without realising it. This guide explains what it actually is.

Artificial Intelligence is one of the most discussed, debated and misunderstood technologies of the modern era. It appears in news headlines daily, is the subject of both enormous excitement and serious concern, and is already present in tools that billions of people use every day, often without realising it.

1.What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence refers to the ability of a computer system to perform tasks that would normally require human intelligence — understanding language, recognising images, making decisions, solving problems, learning from experience, and generating creative content.

The term was coined in 1956 by the American computer scientist John McCarthy, who defined it as "the science and engineering of making intelligent machines." Since then, the field has grown from a theoretical curiosity into one of the most consequential areas of technology in history.

Example

A key distinction: AI does not "think" in the way humans do. It processes data according to mathematical patterns learned from vast amounts of training information. What appears to be understanding or reasoning is the result of extraordinarily sophisticated pattern recognition.

2.How Does AI Actually Work?

Most modern AI systems are built on a technique called machine learning — a method by which a computer improves its performance on a task by learning from data, rather than being explicitly programmed with rules.

Consider an AI system trained to recognise photographs of cats. Rather than being given a list of rules, the system is shown millions of images labelled as either "cat" or "not cat." Over time, it identifies mathematical patterns that distinguish cats from other things — and applies those patterns to new images it has never seen before.

A more advanced form of machine learning, called deep learning, uses artificial neural networks — mathematical structures loosely inspired by the way neurons connect in the human brain. Deep learning is responsible for many of the most impressive recent advances in AI, including speech recognition, language translation, and image generation.

3.Narrow AI and General AI

It is important to distinguish between two very different concepts that are sometimes confused:

TypeAlso Known AsWhat It Means
Narrow AIWeak AIDesigned to perform one specific task only — e.g. a chess AI, a spam filter, or a voice assistant. Can vastly outperform humans at that one task, but cannot transfer the ability to anything else.
General AIStrong AI, AGIA hypothetical system able to understand, learn and apply intelligence across any task, matching human capability across all domains. No general AI exists today.

Every AI system in use today — including the most advanced language models and image generators — is a form of narrow AI. Whether and when general AI might be developed remains one of the most debated questions in technology and philosophy.

4.Where AI is Already Being Used

AI is already embedded in many aspects of daily life, often in ways that are invisible to the user:

  • Search engines — use AI to rank results, understand the intent behind queries, and personalise results
  • Recommendation systems — on platforms such as YouTube, Netflix and Spotify, AI analyses habits to suggest content
  • Navigation apps — use AI to predict traffic, calculate optimal routes and update journeys in real time
  • Healthcare — AI systems analyse medical images, assist in diagnosis, and predict patient outcomes
  • Language tools — grammar checkers, translation apps, and large language models used in AI assistants
  • Fraud detection — banks use AI to identify unusual patterns in transactions instantly

5.The Promise and the Questions AI Raises

The potential benefits of AI are significant. In medicine, AI tools are already detecting certain cancers from scans more accurately than human specialists. In climate science, AI models help predict extreme weather more precisely. In education, AI can personalise learning to individual students' needs.

At the same time, AI raises serious questions that societies are only beginning to grapple with — job displacement as AI automates tasks, bias in systems trained on historical data that reflects past inequalities, privacy concerns from collecting vast amounts of personal data, and questions of accountability when an AI system makes a consequential mistake.

Real-World Example

A hospital uses an AI system trained on thousands of past scans to help radiologists spot early signs of cancer. The AI flags areas of concern faster than a human alone could review — but a qualified doctor always makes the final diagnosis. This is a common pattern across AI applications today: the technology assists and accelerates human judgement, rather than fully replacing it.

A Closing Thought

Artificial Intelligence is neither the all-knowing robot of science fiction nor a simple calculator with a friendly interface. It is a powerful, rapidly developing technology that is already reshaping the world — and understanding what it is and how it works is becoming a fundamental part of being an informed citizen.
