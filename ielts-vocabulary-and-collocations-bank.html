Education & Learning

Core Vocabulary

TermMeaningExample
curriculumthe subjects and content taught in a course of studyThe school recently updated its curriculum to include more computer science.
literacythe ability to read and writeImproving literacy rates remains a major goal in many developing countries.
rote learningmemorising through repetition rather than understandingCritics argue that rote learning discourages critical thinking.
vocationalrelating to skills needed for a particular jobVocational training gives students practical skills for the workplace.
tuitionthe fee paid for instruction, or teaching itselfUniversity tuition has risen sharply over the past decade.
undergraduatea student studying for a first degreeShe's currently an undergraduate studying biology.
accreditationofficial recognition that a course meets required standardsThe programme recently received full accreditation.
extracurricularactivities outside the normal curriculumExtracurricular activities like debate club build valuable soft skills.
plagiarismcopying someone else's work and presenting it as your ownUniversities take plagiarism extremely seriously.
distance learningstudying remotely rather than attending in personDistance learning became far more common after 2020.

Key Collocations

  • acquire knowledgeStudents acquire knowledge more effectively through active discussion.
  • academic achievementAcademic achievement often depends on more than natural ability alone.
  • higher educationAccess to higher education has expanded significantly worldwide.
  • lifelong learningEmployers increasingly value a commitment to lifelong learning.
  • drop out of schoolFinancial pressure is one common reason students drop out of school.
  • sit an examThousands of students will sit an exam this week.
  • pursue a degreeShe decided to pursue a degree in environmental science.
  • broaden one's horizonsStudying abroad can broaden a student's horizons considerably.

Environment & Climate Change

Core Vocabulary

TermMeaningExample
sustainabilitymeeting needs without depleting resources for the futureSustainability has become a core value for many businesses.
carbon footprintthe amount of carbon dioxide produced by activitiesFlying frequently significantly increases a person's carbon footprint.
deforestationthe clearing of forests, usually for other land useDeforestation continues to threaten biodiversity in tropical regions.
renewableable to be replenished naturally, not depletedRenewable energy sources include wind, solar, and hydroelectric power.
emissionssubstances, especially gases, released into the airVehicle emissions are a major contributor to urban air pollution.
biodiversitythe variety of plant and animal life in an areaCoral reefs support extraordinary levels of biodiversity.
ecosystema community of living things and their environmentPollution can disrupt an entire ecosystem, not just a single species.
conservationthe protection of natural resourcesConservation efforts have helped several species recover from near extinction.
droughta prolonged period of insufficient rainfallThe region experienced its worst drought in decades.
greenhouse gasgases that trap heat in the atmosphereCarbon dioxide is the most commonly discussed greenhouse gas.

Key Collocations

  • tackle climate changeGovernments worldwide are under pressure to tackle climate change.
  • reduce emissionsThe policy aims to reduce emissions by a third within a decade.
  • raise awarenessThe campaign was designed to raise awareness of plastic pollution.
  • natural resourcesRapid industrialisation has placed enormous strain on natural resources.
  • environmentally friendlyMore consumers are choosing environmentally friendly products.
  • global warmingGlobal warming is causing sea levels to rise steadily.
  • renewable energyInvestment in renewable energy has grown substantially in recent years.
  • endangered speciesPoaching remains a serious threat to several endangered species.

Technology & Media

Core Vocabulary

TermMeaningExample
artificial intelligencecomputer systems performing tasks that normally require human intelligenceArtificial intelligence is increasingly used in medical diagnosis.
algorithma set of rules a computer follows to solve a problemSocial media platforms use algorithms to decide what content users see.
connectivitythe state of being connected, especially digitallyImproved connectivity has transformed remote working.
cybersecurityprotection of computer systems from digital attacksCybersecurity has become a major concern for businesses of all sizes.
bandwidththe capacity for data transfer on a networkStreaming video requires significantly more bandwidth than browsing text.
obsoleteno longer used because something newer existsMany older devices become obsolete within just a few years.
innovationa new method, idea, or productThe company is known for constant technological innovation.
automationthe use of machines to do tasks previously done by peopleAutomation has changed manufacturing dramatically over the past century.
misinformationfalse or inaccurate information, often spread unintentionallyMisinformation can spread rapidly across social media platforms.
digital dividethe gap between those with and without reliable technology accessThe digital divide became especially visible during remote schooling.

Key Collocations

  • cutting-edge technologyThe lab is developing cutting-edge technology for renewable batteries.
  • rely on technologyModern businesses rely heavily on technology to function efficiently.
  • keep pace with (change)Regulations often struggle to keep pace with rapid technological change.
  • data breachThe company suffered a major data breach last year.
  • streamline a processThe new software helps streamline the entire application process.
  • widespread useThe widespread use of smartphones has changed how people communicate.
  • address a problemEngineers are working to address the problem of battery life.
  • groundbreaking researchGroundbreaking research in this field could transform healthcare.

Work & Employment

Core Vocabulary

TermMeaningExample
workforceall the people who work in a company, industry, or countryAutomation is reshaping the global workforce.
redundancyloss of a job because it's no longer neededThe company announced significant redundancies last month.
entrepreneura person who starts and runs a businessShe became a successful entrepreneur after leaving university.
remunerationpayment for work or servicesRemuneration packages often include benefits beyond salary alone.
probationa trial period at the start of a new jobNew employees typically complete a three-month probation period.
flexitimea system allowing flexible working hoursFlexitime has become popular among companies seeking to boost morale.
burnoutextreme physical or mental exhaustion from workBurnout is an increasingly recognised workplace health issue.
freelanceworking independently rather than for one employerMany young professionals now choose to freelance rather than take permanent roles.
qualificationa skill or achievement that makes someone suitable for a roleA relevant qualification is often required for entry-level positions.
work-life balancethe balance between working time and personal timeEmployees increasingly prioritise work-life balance over salary alone.

Key Collocations

  • climb the career ladderShe worked hard to climb the career ladder within the firm.
  • meet a deadlineThe team worked overtime to meet the project deadline.
  • job satisfactionJob satisfaction depends on more than salary alone.
  • take on responsibilityNew managers must quickly learn to take on responsibility.
  • make redundantHundreds of workers were made redundant during the restructuring.
  • gain experienceInternships are a valuable way to gain experience early in a career.
  • competitive salaryThe role offers a competitive salary and strong benefits package.
  • team playerEmployers consistently look for candidates who are strong team players.

Health & Lifestyle

Core Vocabulary

TermMeaningExample
sedentaryinvolving little physical movementA sedentary lifestyle is linked to numerous long-term health risks.
obesitythe condition of being significantly overweightRates of childhood obesity have risen in many countries.
immune systemthe body's defence against diseaseA balanced diet helps support a healthy immune system.
chronicpersisting over a long timeChronic conditions require ongoing rather than short-term treatment.
wellbeingthe state of being comfortable, healthy, and happyMental wellbeing is now widely recognised as equally important as physical health.
malnutritiona lack of proper nutritionMalnutrition remains a serious problem in several regions of the world.
preventivedesigned to stop something happening in advancePreventive healthcare can reduce long-term treatment costs significantly.
addictiona strong, harmful dependency on somethingAddiction to smartphones is a growing concern among researchers.
life expectancythe average length of time a person is expected to liveLife expectancy has risen substantially over the past century.
holisticconsidering the whole person, not just symptomsMany clinics now favour a more holistic approach to treatment.

Key Collocations

  • lead a healthy lifestyleRegular exercise is essential for anyone hoping to lead a healthy lifestyle.
  • maintain a balanced dietIt can be difficult to maintain a balanced diet while travelling frequently.
  • cope with stressEffective techniques for coping with stress vary from person to person.
  • boost the immune systemVitamin C is often claimed to boost the immune system.
  • get sufficient sleepTeenagers rarely get sufficient sleep during term time.
  • suffer from (an illness)Millions of people worldwide suffer from chronic back pain.
  • reduce the risk ofRegular screening can reduce the risk of late diagnosis.
  • access to healthcareEqual access to healthcare remains a major policy challenge.

Society, Culture & Family

Core Vocabulary

TermMeaningExample
urbanisationthe growth of cities as populations move from rural areasRapid urbanisation has placed enormous pressure on housing supply.
nuclear familya family unit of parents and children onlyThe traditional nuclear family model has become less dominant in many societies.
multiculturalismthe coexistence of diverse cultures within a societyMulticulturalism is often celebrated as a strength of major global cities.
inequalityunfair differences between groups in societyIncome inequality has widened in many countries in recent decades.
heritagetraditions, culture, and history passed down through generationsThe old town is a protected site of significant cultural heritage.
generation gapdifferences in attitudes between older and younger generationsThe generation gap is often visible in attitudes towards technology.
normsaccepted standards of behaviour within a societySocial norms around work have shifted considerably since the pandemic.
assimilateto become part of a different culture or groupIt can take years for newcomers to fully assimilate into a new culture.
demographicrelating to the structure of a populationThe country's ageing demographic presents significant economic challenges.
stereotypean oversimplified, widely held idea about a groupMedia portrayals can reinforce harmful stereotypes.

Key Collocations

  • cultural identityLanguage plays a central role in shaping cultural identity.
  • extended familyIn many cultures, the extended family plays a significant role in raising children.
  • social cohesionShared community spaces can help strengthen social cohesion.
  • bridge the generation gapFamily gatherings can help bridge the generation gap.
  • preserve traditionMany festivals exist specifically to preserve local traditions.
  • rapid urbanisationRapid urbanisation has transformed the skyline of the city entirely.
  • close-knit communityShe grew up in a close-knit community where neighbours knew each other well.
  • shape attitudesUpbringing plays a major role in shaping a person's attitudes.
