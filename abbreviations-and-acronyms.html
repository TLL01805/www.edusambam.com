Abbreviations and Acronyms: Meanings, Rules and Examples

ASAP, e.g., UNESCO, Dr, don't. Learn what common abbreviations mean, the difference between initialisms and acronyms, and the rules for using them correctly.

ASAP, e.g., UNESCO, Dr, don't, exam. Abbreviations are everywhere: in letters, news, textbooks, signs and messages. Used correctly, they save time and space. Used wrongly, they confuse readers and lose marks. This guide explains the different kinds of abbreviation, the rules for punctuation, capital letters and plurals, and gives 212 common abbreviations with their full forms.

Its main part is organised by type: Latin abbreviations, titles and everyday abbreviations, units of measurement, initialisms, acronyms, text abbreviations, contractions and clipped words. It also covers common mistakes, popular myths about acronyms, key points, ten practice questions and a teacher activity.

It connects with Parts of Speech, Formal vs. Informal English, Punctuation Rules and Common Errors in English. Spelling in this guide follows British English.

1.What Is an Abbreviation?

An abbreviation is a shortened form of a word or phrase. The word comes from the Latin abbreviare, meaning "to shorten". There are several kinds, and the difference between them matters for punctuation and pronunciation.

TypeWhat it isExamples
AbbreviationA shortened word, often ending with a full stop in some stylesProf., approx., Jan.
InitialismInitial letters, each said separatelyBBC, UN, CEO, MP
AcronymInitial letters, pronounced as a wordNASA, UNESCO, NATO, laser
ContractionA word or words shortened by leaving out letters, shown with an apostrophecan't, I'm, o'clock
Clipped wordA word cut short in everyday speechexam, gym, photo, flu
Blend (portmanteau)Two words joined, with parts of eachbrunch (breakfast + lunch), smog (smoke + fog)

Some acronyms have become ordinary words and are written in lower case: radar, laser, scuba and sonar.

2.Rules for Using Abbreviations

RuleExplanation and example
Write in full the first timeGive the full form with the abbreviation in brackets: the World Health Organization (WHO). Use the short form afterwards.
Full stops (British style)No full stop when the last letter of the word is included: Mr, Mrs, Dr, St, Ltd. Use a full stop when the word is cut short: Prof., Capt., approx.
Full stops (American style)American English usually uses a full stop after titles: Mr., Mrs., Dr.
No full stops in initialisms and acronymsBBC, UN, NASA, MP (older style used B.B.C.)
Capital lettersInitialisms and acronyms of names are usually capitals: UNICEF. Common-word acronyms are lower case: radar, laser.
PluralsAdd -s without an apostrophe: CDs, MPs, PhDs. Units do not change: 5 km, 10 kg.
a or anChoose by sound, not by spelling: an MP (em-pee), an SOS, a UN official (you-en), a UNESCO report.
Avoid in formal writingWrite for example and and so on in essays, and avoid text abbreviations such as BTW and LOL.
Do not start a sentence with a symbol or most abbreviationsWrite Doctor Khan arrived, or Dr Khan arrived, but not e.g. fruit at the start.

11.E.g. or I.e.? Etc. and Other Confusions

AbbreviationUse it toExample
e.g.give one or more examplesBring stationery, e.g. pens and rulers.
i.e.explain or restateBring a blue pen, i.e. not a black one.
etc.show that a list continuesBring pens, pencils, rulers, etc.
et al.show that there are other authorsSmith et al. (2020)
a.m. / p.m.show time before or after noonThe class starts at 9 a.m.
A.D. / B.C.date years from the birth of ChristThe fort was built in A.D. 1100.

A helpful trick: e.g. means "example given", and i.e. means "in essence".

12.Words That Look Like Acronyms but Are Not

Many popular stories claim that common words began as acronyms. Most of these stories are myths.

WordThe mythThe truth
SOSIt means "Save Our Souls".The letters were chosen because the Morse code pattern was simple to send and recognise. They do not stand for anything.
newsIt means North, East, West, South.It is simply the plural of new.
tipIt means "to insure promptness".The origin is not an acronym.
poshIt means "port out, starboard home".There is no good evidence for this story.
OKIt has a single agreed meaning.Its origin is debated; many dictionaries trace it to a joking misspelling in nineteenth-century America.

MistakeCorrectWhy
and etc.etc.Et already means "and".
ATM machineATMThe M already stands for machine.

HIV virusHIVThe V already stands for virus.
RSVP pleaseRSVPIt already means "please reply".
5 kms5 kmUnit abbreviations do not take -s.
CD'sCDsDo not use an apostrophe for a plural.
a MP, an UN officialan MP, a UN officialChoose a or an by sound.
Mr. Khan (in British style)Mr KhanNo full stop when the last letter is included.
using i.e. for a list of examplesuse e.g.i.e. explains; e.g. gives examples.
writing BTW in an essayby the way, or leave it outText abbreviations are informal.
its/it's confusionits (belonging), it's (it is)An apostrophe shows a contraction.
Key points to remember
  • An abbreviation is a shortened form; initialisms are said letter by letter and acronyms as words.
  • Write the full form first, then the abbreviation in brackets.
  • In British style, do not use a full stop when the last letter of the word is included (Dr, Mr, St).
  • Plurals take -s with no apostrophe (CDs, MPs); units do not change (5 km).
  • Choose a or an by sound: an MP, a UN report.
  • Do not repeat the meaning: ATM machine, PIN number.
  • Use e.g. for examples and i.e. for explanations.
  • Avoid text abbreviations in formal writing.
For teachers and parents

Activity: give students a list of ten abbreviations from the tables and ask them to write the full form from memory, then to use each correctly in a sentence. A matching game with abbreviation cards and full-form cards works well. For older students, ask them to find five abbreviations in a news article and to explain whether each is an initialism or an acronym.

Please note: this guide uses British style for punctuation. Some abbreviations are written differently in other countries, and new ones appear all the time, so students should check a dictionary or style guide when in doubt.
